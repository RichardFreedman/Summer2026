Programme

PART I

Part Song—"Dawn in the Forest"
C. Jos. Brambach

Liedertafel

O! Sweet May night, so calm, so still,
    All forest tones are sleeping,
Save voice of yonder purling rill
    That o'er the stones is leaping.

O! Sweet May night, so calm, so still,
    The flowers in their dreaming are swayed by the breeze,
    Of golden sunlight breathe the trees,
And sweet sleep shrouds vale and hill.

Hark! 'tis a blackbird's sweet song begun;
    The slumbering world is waking.
Arouse thee, to welcome the morning sun;
    The trees their boughs are shaking.

The lark soars up to the bright'ning skies,
Pouring forth song even as he flies,
    Rejoicing that night is gone!

And with the fuller glow of day,
    The morning bells are ringing.
The burden of their fervent lay,
    Our heart with them is singing.

This is their burden, this their song,
That fills all the world, as it flows along:—
    "Praise be to God!  Praise be to God alway!"

Cantata—"The Nun of Hidaros"

Dudley Buck

Liedertafel

(Tenor Solo: Mr. Charles Saunders)

In the convent of Drontheim,
Alone in her chamber
Knelt Astrid the Abbess,
At midnight, adoring,
Beseeching, entreating,
The Virgin and Mother.

She heard in the silence
The voice of one speaking
Without in the darkness,
In gusts of the night-wind
Now louder; now nearer,
Now lost in the distance.

The voice of a stranger
It seemed as she listened,
Of some one who answered,
Beseeching, imploring,
A cry from afar off
She could not distinguish.

The voice of Saint John,
The beloved disciple,
Who wandered and waited
The Master's appearance,
Alone in the darkness,
Unsheltered and friendless.

"It is accepted,
The angry defiance,
The challenge of battle!
It is accepted.
But not with the weapons
Of war that thou wieldest!"

=== Page 10 ===
"Cross against corselet,
Love against hatred,
Peace-cry for war-cry!
Patience is powerful:
He that o'ercometh
Hath dower o'er the nations!

"As torrents in summer,
Half dried in their channels,
Suddenly rise though the
Sky is still cloudless—
For rain has been falling
Far off at their fountains—

"So hearts that are fainting
Grow full to o'erflowing,
And they that behold it
Marvel, and know not
That God at their fountains
Far off has been raining!

"Stronger than steel
Is the sword of the spirit;
Swifter than arrows
The light of the truth is;
Greater than anger
Is love, and subdueth.

"Thou art a phantom,
A shape of the sea mist,
A shape of the brumal
Rain, and the darkness
Fearful and formless;
Day dawns, and thou art not!

"The dawn is not distant,
Nor is the night starless;
Love is eternal;
God is still God, and
His faith shall not fail us!
Christ is eternal!"

PART II

Serenade—"Sleep, Dear One, Sleep"                DR. TORRANCE
Liedertafel

SLEEP, dear one, sleep, may thy rest be unbroken,
    Save by pure thoughts wrapt in harmony divine,
Visions of night, heavenly voices, words unspoken,
    Call us to meet in devotion at thy shrine.
        Sleep, dear one, sleep.

Dream, fair one, dream, while we sing in sweet numbers,
    Angels of light hover round thee ever near,
Nought can approach to disturb thy peaceful slumbers,
    Guarded from harm by thy faithful watchers here.
        Dream, fair one, dream.

Wake! loved one, wake; lo! the day breaks in splendour,
    Earth's fairest flowers are responsive to the call,
Rise on our night, while this homage we would render.
    Be thou our sun, pouring brightness over all.
        Wake! loved one, wake.

Part Song "O World, thou art wondrous fair"  F. Hiller

(Soprano Solo: Mrs. Palmer)

Liedertafel

O world, thou art wondrous fair,
O how fair, so fair, so fair!
    The birds they all know it well,
While over the woods they flutter,
And joyful sweet carols they utter,
    To the heav'ns their melodies swell.

O world, thou art wondrous fair,
O how fair, so fair, so fair!
    They know it the waters all,
Reflected in them all nature
Stands, mountain and mead and creature,
    And the clouds as they float and fall.

The minstrels and painters know it,
    So does many a shepherd boy,
They copy all and feel it,
And those who sing reveal it,
    Their hearts so full of joy.

Violoncello Solo—
"Romance, Souvenir d'Ems" Piatti
Mr. George E. Howard

Song—"An Evening Song" Blumenthal
Mr. Charles Saunders

Part Song—"The Long Day Closes" Sullivan
Liedertafel
No star is o'er the lake
    Its pale watch keeping,
The moon is half awake,
    Through gray mist creeping.
The last red leaves fall round
    The porch of roses,
The clock hath ceased to sound,
    The long day closes.

Sit by the silent hearth
    In calm endeavour
To count the sounds of mirth,
    Now dumb for ever.
Heed not how hope believes,
    And fate disposes:
Shadow is round the eaves,
    The long day closes.

The lighted windows dim
    Are fading slowly.
The fire that was so trim
    Now quivers lowly.
Go to the dreamless bed
    Where grief reposes;
Thy book of toil is read,
    The long day closes.

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced.

This is the Fifth Concert of our Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only.

Intending Subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s.; Class C, £1 11s. 6d. Entrance fee to each Class, 10s. 6d.

Class A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 tickets.

Class B.—Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 tickets.

Class C.—Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 tickets.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.
