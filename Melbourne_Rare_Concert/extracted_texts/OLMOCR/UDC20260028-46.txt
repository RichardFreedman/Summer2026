=== Page 6 ===
Programme.

Under the Baton of
Mr. August Siede.

Part I.

OVERTURE—
"Fidelio"
The Orchestra. Beethoven.

"The Migration of the Normans"
Max Bruch.
Soloist—Signor A. Rebottaro.

Liedertafel and Orchestra.

THE evening approaches and the autumnal breeze is blowing;
Hoarfrost encircles the firs;
O crosier and book and monks prayer;
We must all leave here (go away from here).

The native country is getting dark, dusky and cold;
The holy springs are running turbidly.
Already the axe is flashing to fell thee
Thou green forest, round which the gods are hovering.

And we depart silently, a beaten army;
Extinguished are our stars;
O Iceland, icy rock of the sea,
Rise up in mighty distance!

Rise up and receive our equipped race!
The old gods, the old right,
The old Normans
Upon rostrate ships come swimming.

Where the volcano is blazing embers are falling,
Stormy waves surround the shores with foam;
Upon thee, thou defying extremity of the world
We will dream away the winter night.

=== Page 7 ===
ARIA—
"Und ob die Wolke sie ver-hülle"
(DER FREISCHUTZ) Weber.
Miss Hesketh Jones.

"NÄNIE"
(SCHILLER) Johannes Brahms.
Op. 82.
For Chorus of Mixed Voices and Orchestra.
Liedertafel,
Associated Ladies' Choir and Orchestra.

AND the beautiful too, must die!
    Even the beautiful die,
What men and gods has conquered, melts not the iron heart,
Melts not the stygian Jove.
Only once, for love! the Monarch of Shadows relented,
But at the threshold called back.
    Called he back his gift!
Aphrodite staunches not the wound,
Which in the lovely body, the Cruel Boar has torn.
Nor could mother immortal save the godly hero falling at the gate of Troy,
    Falling, his fate fulfilled!
But from the sea she arises, with the daughters of Nereus,
And in loud lamenting, mourns for the son now glorified.
    See, how mourn the gods!
    See, how mourn the goddesses all!
That the beautiful Fades!
Mourn that the Highest dies!
And a song of lament from heart of the loved is glorious!
    Ah! to go songless and silent to Orcus dark reign!
And a song of lament from heart of the loved is glorious!

Part 2.

Symphony. No. 2 in D.
Beethoven,
Adagio Molto—Allegro con brio—Larghetto—
Scherzo (Allegro)—Finale (Allegro molto).
The Orchestra.

THE CATHEDRAL SCENE
from "FAUST"
Schumann.
(Service, Organ and Singing. Gretchen amongst the crowd. Evil Spirit behind Gretchen.)
Miss Hesketh Jones (Gretchen),
Signor A. Rebottaro (Evil Spirit),
Liedertafel,
Associated Ladies' Choir and Orchestra.

Evil Spirit—
How diff'rent, Gretchen, was it
When thou cam'st with pure heart
Here to the altar,
Thy well thumb'd little book didst clasp,
Thy pray'r's soft lisping;
Thy thoughts half playful,
Half turned to Heaven.
Gretchen,
Where now thy thoughts?
And in thy bosom
What misdeed hast thou?
Ist for thy mother's soul thou'rt praying,
Who through thee, to never ending pain,
Slept on and on?
Upon the threshold, whose the blood?
What stirs beneath thy heart?
Hath it not dawning Life
That scares itself and thee
With its forboding presence!

=== Page 9 ===
Gretchen—
Woe! Woe!
Could I from the thoughts escape,
That pierce through my being
And, accusing me,
Haunt me still!

Chorus—
Dies irae, dies illa,
Solvent sæculum in favilla.

Evil Spirit—
Tremble thou!
For the trumpet sounds!
The graves are quaking!
And from its ashy rest,
Thy heart,
For flaming tortures
Afresh created,
Starts forth;

Gretchen—
Would I were hence!
'Twould seem as though the organ's tone
Could hinder my breathing;
The singing
Thrills my heart with torture!

Chorus—
Judex ergo cum sedebit,
Quidquid latet ad parebit,
Nil inultum remanebit.

Gretchen—
Oppress'd I feel!
The cluster'd pillars
Imprison me,
And the groined roof
Whelms me—Air!

Evil Spirit—
Ah, hide thee! Sin and shame
Are never hidden.
Air? Light?
Woe's thee.

Chorus—
Quid sum miser tunc dicturus?
Quem prætonum rogaturus,
Cum vix justus sit securus?

Evil Spirit—
Their faces from thee
The blessed turn away,
The pure from thee withhold
Their hands with shudd'ring!
Woe!

Chorus—
Quid sum miser tunc dicturus?

Gretchen—
Friend your help!
Or I faint.

Notice to Members

Choir Rehearsals are held on Monday and Orchestral Rehearsals on Tuesday evenings, at 8 p.m., at Society's Rooms, Independent Hall, Russell Street.

The thanks of the Society are due to Messrs. W. H. Glen & Co., Collins Street, for use of Box Office, and to the Conservatorium of Music, Albert Street, East Melbourne, for loan of Orchestral Parts.

Ladies and Gentlemen wishing to join the Society, either as Subscribing or Performing Members, will please communicate with the Hon. Secretary, c/o Messrs. Glen & Co., Collins Street, who will be pleased to forward all information required.
