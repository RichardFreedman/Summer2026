第2回 パシフィック現代音楽祭 開催を歓迎する

태평양을 함께 애워싼 이웃들이 모여 음악으로 화합하는 제2회 태평양 현대음악제가 우리 서울에서 열리게 된 것을 우리 서울 가족들은 대단히 기쁘게 생각합니다.
천만 시민을 대표하여 여러분을 환영합니다. 아시아 동쪽에 음악을 사랑하는 민족이 있고 그 민족은 매우 영국하고 고상한 품성을 지녔다고 말하던 이웃이 중국의 哲人 공자였다고 합니다.
그 동쪽의 민족이란 말할 것도 없이 우리 민족을 음악을 즐기는 것이라고 들었습니다. 창의력이 뛰어나고 예술을 사랑하는 기질 때문에 마음이 트이고 악하지 않게 살아온 우리 한국인은, 언제 어디서나 음악을 즐기고 사랑합니다. 서울 음악의 노래가 지구촌의 깊숙한 오지에도, 망망한 사막과 얼어붙은 동토에도 스며들어 오래오래 불리워지는 것은 우리의 마음이 깃들어 있기 때문입니다.
한국이 낳은 음악의 재능들은 세계무대를 누비고 있습니다. 국제적인 오페라좌를 이끌고 뛰어난 연주로 세계를 감동시키고, 음악을 경영하여 親和를 創出합니다.
미국에서 태평양 음악제를 설립하신 우리의 김병곤 교수도 그런 업적을 남긴 분이라고 생각합니다. 다가오는 미래가 이곳 태평양권을 중심으로 펼쳐지리라는 예측은 세계가 공감하여 외심치 않는 일입니다. 창작예술의 中心軸이 태평양권으로 옮겨 온다는 것은 지극히 자연스런 당위입니다. 서울은 태평양시대의 핵심에 놓인 가장 자격있는 도시입니다. 우아한 古都 그리고 신선한 현대 도시입니다. 정통과 활력으로 섬솟는 인재가 있고 이해력 깊은 청중이 있으며, 적당하고 빈곤하지 않은 시설이 있습니다. 富裕하지는 못해도 賦有가 있는 국력이 뒷받침 하고 있으며 언제나 정성을 다하려는 저희 서울시가 있습니다. 그러한 우리의 자부심인 서울 시립 교향악단이 음악제를 빛내리라 확신 합니다. 성공적인 음악제를 시민을 자랑스럽게 할 것입니다. 좋은 음악제를 위해 멀리 찾아오신 여러분들에게 만족한 마음을 느낄 수 있는 음악제가 되는 "서울 나들이"가 되기를 진심으로 기원합니다. 참가하신 모든분들의 노고를 치하드리며 도와주신 여러분께 감사드립니다.
고맙습니다.

Mayor's Welcome Message

We are honored to host the Pacific Contemporary Music Festival II, an event for which music lovers around the Pacific can get together as a family. I welcome you on behalf of our 10 million Seoul citizens.

Confucius, a saga of the ancient China, once said that there lived a people to the east of China who loved music and who was refined and intelligent. They must have meant our Korean people. Koreans today are open-minded and enjoy music anytime, anywhere, and because of their creative and music loving character, they have done no harm to other peoples. They hymn of the Seoul Olympics carrying our heart still echoes in the far away places, in the vast desert and in the frozen land around the globe.

Many young musicians of Korea are showing their talents all over the world. Leading international opera theater, some move the world with great performances, while some bring harmony to the world, organizing music festivals. I think Prof. Byong-kon Kim who founded the festival in America is a person.

The world of today foresees that in the near future the Pacific nations will play major roles in the world. It goes without speaking that these nations will also play major roles in the creative arts. Seoul, enjoying both ancient elegance and modern freshness, is the most qualified city in the center of the Pacific stage. There are people with tradition and liveliness, audience with deep understanding, and sufficient facilities. Seoul is not affluent, but is faithfully supported by the government, and the city of Seoul will do our best. I can assure you that Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra, the pride of our city, will add magnificence and grandeur to the Festival. And the citizens of Seoul will be proud of a successful festival.

I wish everyone a satisfying festival and a worthy trip to Seoul who came a long way for the festival. I thank all the participants who helped and put in so much efforts.

Thank you.

It is my great honor to host the Second Pacific Contemporary Music Festival here in the City of Daegu. 환영사

제2회 패시픽 현대음악제에 참가해주시는 여러분에게 환영의 인사말을 드리게 된 것을 무한한 기쁨으로 생각하며 또한 자랑스럽게 생각합니다.
캘리포니아주립대학교(로스앤젤레스)는 캘리포니아 남부지역의 복수문화예술에 있어서 급성장을 하고 있는 중심지입니다. 「패시픽 현대음악제」는 서로 현저하게 다른 문화를 가진 사람들 사이를 조명해 보기 위하여 다리를 놓으며, 동시에 혁신적인 예술적 실험을 맞이하는 본 대학교 최고의 위임기구들 중의 하나입니다.
김병곤 박사의 정력적인 리더십 아래, 본 센터는 30개국 이상의 작곡가들로 구성된계끔 성장을 하였으며, 이들 회원들은 학문연구, 창작예술의 시도, 그리고 홀륭한 음악연주에 참여하고 있습니다. 올해의 음악제는 전세계의 절반에 걸친 지역에서 참가하여, 본 대학교의 캠퍼스와, 한국의 서울, 대구 등지에서 음악회와 세미나를 개최하게 되었습니다.
우리는 여러분이, 이 음악제를 즐기시고 동시에 음악제 기간동안 펼쳐지는 풍부한 음악회와 학문의 행사에서 많은 것을 성취하시도록 뜨거운 마음으로 여러분을 초대합니다. 여러분의 참가로 인하여 모든 태평양 지역 국가들을 위한, 보다 큰 이해와 보다 나은 미래를 위한 많은 것들이 쌓아져리라 믿어지지 않습니다.

캘리포니아 주립대학교(로스앤젤레스)총장
제임스 로서 박사

WELCOME MESSAGE

It is with great pleasure and pride that I extend a warm welcome to the participants of the Pacific Contemporary Music Festival.

California State University, Los Angeles is a burgeoning center for the multicultural arts within Southern California. The Pacific Contemporary Music Center is one of the finest examples of the University's commitment to building bridges of illumination among peoples of widely different cultures while, at the same time, embracing innovative artistic experimentation.
Under Dr. Byong-kon Kim's dynamic leadership, the Center has grown to include composers from over 30 countries who participate in scholarly research, creative endeavors and exciting musical performances. This year's Music Festival spans half the globe, with concerts and seminars on the campus of Cal State L.A. and in Seoul and Daegu, Korea.
We invite you to enjoy and benefit from the rich array of music and scholarship presented during the Music Festival. Your participation in this event can build greater understanding of and a better future for all Pacific Rim nations.

Dr. James M. Rosser
President,
California State University, Los Angeles

패시픽 현대음악센터 (PCMC)

비영리적 단체인 「패시픽 현대음악센터」는 캘리포니아 주립대학교(로스엔젤레스)에서 이 학교의 작곡과 교수이고, 국제적인 명성을 얻고 있는 작곡가이며 지휘자, 또한 음악교육자인 김병곤 박사에 의하여 1986년 설립되었다. 본 센터는 환태평양지역 국가들의 작곡가들에 의한 현대음악작품의 연주, 교환, 이해를 촉진시키기 위하여 설립된 국제적인 기구이다. 이 기구는 연주가·메스미디어·학자들과의 연락업무도 담당하고 있으며, 작품·녹음·전기(傳記)·연주정보 등, 여러 분야와의 관계를 유지하고 있다.

PACIFIC CONTEMPORARY MUSIC CENTER

The Pacific Contemporary Music Center, a non-profit organization, was founded in 1986 at California State University, Los Angeles, by Dr. Byong-kon Kim, Professor of Composition and an internationally recognized composer, conductor and educator. The Center is an international organization established to encourage performance, exchange, and awareness of contemporary music by composers of the Pacific Rim countries. It serves as a liaison for performers, mass media, scholars, et al. It maintains a library of members: compositions, sound recordings, biographies and performance information.

베르나르도 펠드만은 멕시코 태생의 작곡가로 1982년 이후 캘리포니아에 살고 있다. 그의 작품은 실내악, 관현악곡, 테이프 음악, 전자음악등 다양하다. 그의 음악은 미국, 멕시코, 유럽, 이스라엘 등지에서 <Orquesta Filarmónica de la Ciudad de Mexico>, <Cal-Arts 20th Century Players>, <New London Contemporary Ensemble>, <New York North/South Consonance>등의 연주단에 의해 연주 되었다.

그는 1987년 로스엔젤레스 축제때 작품을 위촉 받은 바 있으며, 1989년 멕시코시에서 열린 <Foro Internacional de Musica Contemporanea>에서도 작품 위촉을 받았다. 다른 분야의 예술가들과 공동작업한 작품중 타원한 것으로는 디즈니 유선방송을 위해 레이 페타가 제작한 <Cloud Dreamer>, Guillermo Gomez Pena의 <Orpheus and Euridice's Cumbia>를 위한 반주음악, 또 블록 연주자 주디 골드워터, 무용가 리 카밀로 등과 공동작업한 <A Tribute to Frida Kahlo>를 들 수 있다. 그는 <Churchill Films>회사를 위해 도큐멘터리 음악을 만든 바 있다. 펠드만은 <DAFCA>, <Ahmanson Foundation>, <ASCAP>, <Meet the Composer, Inc.>등으로부터 상을 받았다.

그는 California Institute of the Arts에서 가르쳐 왔으며, 현재 College of the Canyons의 음악과장으로 재직하고 있다. 또한 미국 전자 음향학회의 로스엔젤레스 지부장직도 맡고 있다.

ONIRICA: 현실세계에서는 이룩할 수 없는 많은 것들이 우리의 꿈속에서 이루어지니 꿈이란 얼마나 불가사의 하면서도 유익한 것인가? 꿈을 꾸는 사람은 현실세계에서의 이성적인 제약들을 초월하여 미지의 세계 속에서 현재와 과거의 벽을 넘나들며 여행하는 것이다.
눈을 감는 동시에 그녀는 결코 속이 더 큰 환상의 세계를 통과하며, 친숙한 얼굴과 낯선 얼굴들이 작가없는 연극의 주인공으로 둘러싸여 그녀를 현혹시킨다. 그녀는 아무도 쓰지않은 그 연극의 관객이자 동시에 주인공인 것이다. 그녀는 상처받고, 사랑받고, 모욕받는다. 그녀는 눈을 감아도 볼 수 있고, 귀가 없이도 들을 수 있다. 꿈에서 깨어남과 동시에 그녀는 자신이 꿈속에서 살았던 인생들에 대해 생각에 잠길지 모른다. 아니면 꿈속에서의 일들을 잊기로 하고 현실세계에서 자신에게 다가올 하루하루를 설계할지 모른다.

Bernardo Feldman is a Mexican composer who has lived in California since 1982. His works include music for chamber and symphonic ensembles as well as compositions for tape and live electronics. His music has been performed throughout the United States, Mexico, Europe and Israel by major ensembles such as the Orquesta Filarmónica de la Ciudad de Mexico, the Cal-Arts 20th Century Players, the New London Contemporary Ensemble and the New York North/South Consonance.

He has received commissions to write for the Los Angeles Festival in 1987 as well as for the Foro Internacional de Musica Contemporanea in Mexico City in 1989. Prominent among his collaborations with artists of other media are Cloud Dreamer, an animated film for the Disney Cable Channel by Ray Huerta; the incidental music for Guillermo Gomez Pena's Orpheus and Euridic's Cumbia and A Tribute to Frida Kahlo with flutist Judy Goldwater and dancer Lee Carrillo. He has also scored several documentaries for Churchill Films.

Mr. Feldman has been the recipient of several awards from DAFCA, the Ahmanson Foundation, ASCAP, and Meet the Composer, Inc.

He has been a faculty member at the California Institute of the Arts and is currently head of the Music Department at College of the Canyons. He is also president of the Los Angeles Chapter of the United Society of Electro-Acoustic Music in the States (SEAMUS-L.A.).

ONIRICA: Nightmares and dreams possess wondrous advantages over reality; by transcending the constraints of reason, the dreamer travels beyond unforeseen places in which he confuses Now with a multitude of Yesterdays. As she closes her eyes, escaping through fantastic worlds that are larger on the inside than they are on the outside, hazy images of familiar and foreign faces dazzle her senses by transforming themselves into the characters of a play that nobody ever wrote and for which she is both audience and protagonist, all at once. She is hurt, loved or disdained. She can see and hear, although her eyes are shut and her ears slumber. Perhaps, as she awakes, she will wonder about the many lives she just lived or she may decide to forget them as they secretly continue to shape her days to come.

김정길 (金正吉): 1962년 서울대학교 음악대학 작곡과를 졸업하고 1972년 서독의 Hannover Staatliche Hochschule für Musik의 미스터 클래스를 수료했다. 매우 활발한 작곡활동을 하고 있는 그는 수많은 음악제에서 작품이 위촉되어 연주되었다.

Chung-Gil Kim has been active as a composer and music critic since his graduation from the College of Music at Seoul National University in 1962. Mr. Kim also underwent advanced study in Composition at the Staatliche Hochschule für Musik in Hanover, West Germany. At present, he is on the faculty at the College of Music, Seoul National University.

GO PUNG: The Wbang-jong, Eb, Choong-lyu, Ab, and Imjang, Bb, are often used as core-tones in both kinds of traditional Korean music: music for the Court, and folk music. These three notes assume a similar function in this composition. An extremely slow-moving melody with a harmonic ostinato reflects the peaceful and dignified mood of Court music in the first movement, Hyang-Hap (Incense Case). The second movement, Na-Mak Shin (Wooden Shoes), establishes a folkish mood with a minimalistically repeated rhythmic pattern derived from the "Taryong." MOUNTAINScape FOR ORCHESTRA was written while the composer was a visiting scholar at California State University at Los Angeles in 1988. It requires a large orchestra and a great amount of percussion.

The work's principal elements are "sustaining tones" in the horizontal domain, and "complex sounds" accumulated with various tones in the vertical domain. Groups of tones are formed by combining overlapping verticals with sustained tones, resulting in the accumulation major and minor seconds as well as the more complex sounds of fourths and fifths. Volumes of vertical sound piled up by various instruments progressing step by step are obtained by the symmetry between stress and timbre. In addition to these combinations, frequent use of percussion supplies a magnificent contrast and intensity among sustained tones. The central idea of this work is based on the beauty and the characteristics of Korea.

The composer writes: "I kept in mind yearning for the mountain, the mystery of the mountainscape, and the beauty and immenseness of the mountain as well."

신포니에타는 서울 바로크오케스트라의 委屬에 依해 1987년 3月에 完成되어 金受團長과 團員에게 현정된 曲이다. 여기에서 신포니에타(小交響曲)란 이름은 "小"規模란 뜻이 아니고, 作曲家의 本樂團에 對한 感情을 表現하는 愛稱의 뜻으로 사용된 것이다. 이曲은 3樂章構成, 18分의 演奏時間을 갖는 중장하고 큰 曲이다. 16名의 獨奏者를 前提로作曲되었다.

PROJECTION FOR STRING QUARTET which was commissioned for the 1970 "Festival of the Arts of this Century," was premiered in Hawaii by members of the Juilliard Ensemble. The principle which I used in this composition is one by which many sounds may be equated with one sound and vice-versa. This may be exemplified in the following way: Since any single sound has a component of complex sounds (its harmonic spectrum) even a single sound has its own texture.

In Projection, I developed several new playing techniques, one of which is the rubbing of the bow up and down the deflected strings. Besides the acquisition of new sound materials by these techniques, my main concern in the composition of this piece was with forming a tightly-knit, homophonic sound through the integration created by blending instruments that use identical techniques simultaneously."

SERENADE FOR CHAMBER ORCHESTRA was composed in 1983 and completed at Montecito, California. The work is scored for woodwinds in pairs, four brass, one percussionist, piano and string orchestra.

The opening motive of five pitches (F, C, E flat, C sharp, A) serves as a unifying element throughout the entire work. Each of the five movements, in turn, begins and ends with one of those pitches; thus movement I begins and ends with F, movement II with C and so on. The Serenade is further organized in a chain-like manner with each movement being based on some motivic element (often secondary) taken from the previous movement. Thus the second movement (Visione) is based on elements of the first (Preludio), while the third (Interludio) is in turn based on material borrowed from the second. The Finale employs material from the fourth movement (Musica notturna) and serves to complete the circle as is also related to the first. In addition, the brief Interludio which is initially rather abruptly cut short, now returns as a coda to the Finale and leads to a more conclusive climax.

The Serenade was first conceived and sketched out during a lengthy stay in Venice, Italy. Although the music is in no sense programmatic, the lights and colors of that luminous city did undeniably serve as source of inspiration.

DREAM SEQUENCE was commissioned by Mr. Frank Taplin and was completed on June 17, 1986.
The title Dream Sequence occurred to me some time after I had finished writing the piece. It refers to any passage in a play or movie where a dream is portrayed. Such a scene typically comprises a series of events whose succession appears illogical, yet serves to illustrate in some way a protagonist's state of mind. The title seemed to fit the piece because there is no reasonable narrative explanation for why a particular bird-call, fragment of Oriental folk-melody, or other musical detail found its way in. These things were needed; they were part of the dream.
The first movement opens rather feverishly with a long phrase characterized by dynamic surges, unstable texture, and a quick dissolution. This is followed by a halting viola melody, supported intermittently by a low, fitful accompaniment. The development of this idea is interrupted, but at the same time answered, by the flute, soon joined by oboe and clarinet; whereupon all instruments participate in a

=== Page 34 ===
more sustained drive toward the goal of the first section. This consists of an insistent disjunct passage for the piano in its and subsequent collapse. The middle part of the movement strongly contrasts with all this: it is ushered in by muted strings, and characterized by a reflective English horn melody. Eventually the piano joins the strings and the bass clarinet recalls the English horn melody. Following this, elements derived from the first section reappear in new guises and positions. At the same time these disparate elements are more tightly woven together by means of an overarching melodic line. The climax of this line coincides with the return of the high disjunct music (previously for piano), now played by the piccolo. A coda brings the movement to a close.

The second movement is really a scherzo, but this description ought to be qualified in the following way. A traditional scherzo moves in fast triple time, and the listener is consistently aware of its dance-like quality. In this movement the quick triple meter is always present, but although sometimes in the foreground it is merely latent, owing to the tendency of the music to broaden into a kind of evanescent lyricism, or else to redivide the measure into fours or fives, etc., rather than into threes. The scherzo quality therefore derives as much from shifts of metrical focus as from an evocation of the dance. The "persona" behind the scherzo adopts changing disguises.

The last movement is an adagio, begun and ended by a long oboe melody.

CONCERTO-DIPTYCH for viola (cello), percussion orchestra, celesta and harp(1981) was set for an original cast of performers and consists of two parts without interval—Notturno and Perpetuum Mobile. The composer’s principal idea is to reproduce contrasting emotional states with musical means, as well as to contrast the voice of man’s soul with that of the surrounding world. The first part of the composition (Notturno) is “night music” with typical states of rest and mystery. The melodic phrases of the soloist are as if cloaked with mystic peals and rustles, made by the percussion orchestra with pitched metal instruments.
The second part (Perpetuum mobile) is a toccata with contrasting energetic movements, uneasy nervous rhythms and the use of ostinato principles which counteract the static atmosphere of the notturno. The sensation of anxiety, alarm and effort of today’s life is reproduced with the group of instruments with dry “prickly” sonorousness. The anxious voice of the viola enters into constant dialogue with different instruments, and only at the end of the composition does the state of enlightenment and harmony come. United thematic material, changing through the whole composition,

promotes the sensation of unity of the internal and the external world. The expressive mastery and coloring possibilities of the viola and the accompanying instruments are widely used in the composition.

The premiere of the Concerto-Diptych took place in Cologne, West Germany. The composition was recorded by Melodiya and is published by the publishing house "Sovetsky Compozitor."

PRIDE AND FOOLISHNESS: Five often un-asked questions about Pride and Foolishness:

Q: Why did you call your piece Pride and Foolishness?
A: I named it after the two most important qualities a composer can have while writing music. If you don't try to make the work as well-crafted as your previous musical experience will allow, you can't feel pride in it. If you don't take chances and thus risk appearing foolish, you and your music won't grow and change.

Q: Why did you write Pride and Foolishness?
A: I don't Know. It won't have much effect in the GNP, nor will it solve the problems of homelessness, illiteracy or television. I guess it kept me off the streets.

Q: What could someone listen for during a performance of Pride and Foolishness?
A: There's a melody which is supposed to be quite prominent, and two supporting lines: continuous running notes which move from instrument to instrument, and a punctuating octave figure. These three elements change their overall relationships about half way through the piece.

Son et lumiere ("Sound and Light," a kind of show staged for tourists at historic sites or famous buildings) is an orchestral entertainment whose subject is the play of col-

=== Page 39 ===
ors, bright surfaces, and shimmery textures. I have tried in this music to recapture the elan and immediacy that regular meters and repetitive rhythms make possible—something forbidden during the modernist regime but recently restored in the postmodern work of composers like John Adams, Steve Reich, and others. Throughout its brief nine-minute span, the piece is built almost exclusively of short, busy ostinato figures—my attempt, I suppose, to achieve the rhythmic vitality of minimalism, but without giving in to the over-simple harmonic language that usually comes with it.

Surprisingly, the musical materials seemed determined to shape themselves into an approximation of nineteenth-century sonata form. We hear an introduction, a first theme (Based on triadic broken chords), a second theme (beginning with the flute solo), and a closing theme (led by two piccolos). In a sort of development section, these materials are recombined in new ways, and in a recapitulation, both the first and second themes are recalled more or less intact (part of the second is actually repeated quite literally).

Then, in the coda a second surprise: As if another, different music has been lurking all the while behind the shiny surface, the strings now unexpectedly split off from the rest of the orchestra to assert a new, more passionate, more “serious” voice, transcending the external show of sound and light.

Son et lumiere, commissioned by the Baltimore Symphony Orchestra, was composed between June and December 1988 in Ithaca (N.Y.), in Los Angeles, and at the artists’ colony Yaddo, in Saratoga Springs (N.Y.). David Zinman conducted the first performance in Baltimore on 18 May 1989.

INTERPLAY: The title tells much about the piece: there are three ideas which form the basis for the ensuing interplay, or variations. The term "ideas" is used rather than "themes," because it is the character of each of these ideas, not the actual pitch and rhythmic content that, for the most part, is exploited throughout the piece.

The three ideas are presented in an introductory section. The first idea is stated by way of a cello cadenza, second by flutes, and then, later, strings. My choice of instruments here was shaped in part by my admiration for the playing of the Los Angeles Philharmonic's principal cellist, Ronald Leonard, and for the orchestra's flutists.

While the second idea is still being played, the third enters. It consists simply of four parallel thirds played by an ensemble of three percussionists on vibraphone, orchestra bells and crotales. The exposition of three percussionists on vibraphone, orchestra bells and crotales. The exposition of these ideas concludes with the timpani fragmenting the second idea. The interplay of all this begins immediately thereafter.

Ideas one and two are never again stated completely, but rather, they are fragmented, and it is the elements resulting from that fragmentation which are exploited and varied. The interplay consists not only of the interaction of the elements themselves, but also in the way they are thrown about the orchestra. The word "play" within the title is descriptive of the character of the piece itself.

Interplay was commissioned by the Los Angeles Philharmonic for the occasion of the 80th birthday of Dorothy Chandler, and received its premiere on November 1, 1984, in the Dorothy Chandler Pavilion with Simon Rattle conducting.

PRISMATIC VARIATIONS was completed on August 18, 1983. It was written on commission of Leonard Slatkin, Music Director of the St. Louis Symphony, who with his orchestra gave the first performance of the work on January 28, 1984. It has since been performed by the New York Philharmonic, the Chicago Symphony, the Festival of the Americas Orchestra, and others.

Mr. Erb writes: "Composers are often surprised by the qualities their music presents to the public, but this work will, I think, have a couple of qualities that will be exemplified by the title I have chosen. One thing I have tried to achieve in this piece is to fill the hall with bright, transparent sound. This is accomplished by the orchestra, but it is also enhanced by participants in the audience who are called upon to play, separately or in combination, such instruments as harmonicas, soda bottles and telephone bells. The other quality that this piece should have has to do with the fact that much of the music is repeated during the course of the work, but is altered by being radically reorchestrated for each successive appearance. In other words, some of the variations are simply changes in orchestration and not changes in the shape of the theme—or that of the preceeding variation. This gives the work a quality similar in some ways to a rondo as much as to a theme and variations."

The score calls for two flutes and piccolo, two oboes and English horn, two clarinets and bass clarinet, two bassoons and contrabassoon, four horns, three trumpets, three trombones, tuba, timpani, a large percussion ensemble, three harmonicas, slide whistle, piano, electric piano, electric organ, harp, strings and approximately eighty young people in the audience playing harmonicas, soda bottles, stemmed glasses and telephone bells.

MUSIC FOR PRAGUE 1986 was first performed in its orchestral version on January 31, 1970, with the composer conducting the Munich Philharmonic Orchestra. The premiere of the original version for concert band took Place in Washington, D. C. on January 31, 1989, with Dr. Kenneth Snapp conducting the Ithaca College Concert Band in concert for the Music Educator's National Conference.

The work was commissioned by the Ithaca Band and composed during the summer and fall of 1968 for the capital city of Czechoslovakia. Since then, the work has been per-

=== Page 44 ===
formed by virtually every major American orchestra, as well as by university and community ensembles. In Asia, the Hong Kong Philharmonic and Singapore Symphony have played the work under the composer's baton.

Three main ideas bind the composition together. The first and most important is an old Hussite war song from the 15th century, "Ye Warriors of God and His Law", a symbol of resistance and hope for hundreds of years, whenever fate lay heavy on the Czech nation. It has been utilized by many Czech composers, including Smetana in "My country." The beginning of this religious song is announced very softly in the first movement by the timpani and concludes in a strong unison (Chorale). The song is never used in its entirety.

The second idea is the sound of bells throughout: Prague, named also the city of "Hundreds of Towers," has used its magnificently sounding church bells as calls of distress as well as of victory.

The last idea is a motive of three chords first appearing very softly under the piccolo solo at the beginning of the piece, in flutes, clarinets, and horns. Later it reappears at extremely strong dynamic levels, for example, in the middle of the Aria.

Different techniques of composing as well as orchestration have been used in Music for Prague 1968 and some new sounds explored, such as the percussion section in the Interlude, and at the ending of the work. Much symbolism also appears: in addition to the distress calls in the first movement (Fanfares), the unbroken hope of the Hussite song, sound of bells, or the tragedy (Aria), there is also the bird call at the beginning (piccolo solo), symbol of the liberty which the City of Prague has seen only for moments during its thousand years of existence.

Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra
During the Japanese occupation of Korea (1919–1945), Korean cultural activities across all social strata were oppressed. With the liberation of Korea in 1945 came a great rejuvenation of the undying Korean spirit. Cultural activities flourished again, and music was no exception. The Association of Korean Symphonies was founded on

=== Page 50 ===
September 15, 1945, and the Korean Symphony Orchestra (KSO) was born, predecessor of today's Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra (SPO). In October of 1945, the KSO gave its first concert under the baton of Maestro Chung-Shik Kye at the Sudo Theatre in Seoul. After conducting 26 KSO concerts over the next two years, Maestro Kye stepped down and Weon-Shik Yim was invited to assume leadership. The orchestra soon disbanded, however, for lack of funds.

After a silence lasting several months, the Seoul Symphony Orchestra (SSO) was established in January, 1948. The first subscription concert, conducted by J.S. Kim in February, 1948. After 14 concerts, J.S. Kim was named full-time Music Director, and a support group, the Seoul Symphony Association, was born.

In April of 1950, the SSO acquired instruments and music worth between $40,000 and $50,000 (US) from a former Chief Director of the KSO. All former members of the KSO were also retained. Then the devastating blow struck.

Invasion from the North in June 1950 was a shock felt worldwide. The direct effect on the orchestra was disastrous, as many members were forcibly taken into North Korea and many valuable instruments were destroyed. Once again, the music stopped, but this time the silence was like that following a death knell. Again however, the incredible strength and determination of the Korean people was demonstrated. After an attempt at resurrection of the SSO, it incorporated as the Navy Symphony and Chorus, consisting of capable naval personnel and numbering 105 persons. The inaugural concert in November, 1950, was held at Shi-Gong Hall.

The Navy Symphony and Chorus was renamed the Navy Symphony Orchestra (NSO) and soon increased its membership. The Korean Symphony Association, a new corporation, was set up as a support group, with J.S. Kim as chief Director. In March of 1957, the orchestra made a two-month tour of Hong Kong, Taipei and Saigon, on a goodwill mission. Soon the Seoul Metropolitan Council approved the establishment of an orchestra, and on August 14th of 1957, the Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra (SPO), with its 82 members under the baton of Maestro Kim, was officially founded.

This sensational event was a real turning point for symphonic music in Korea. By 1961, the orchestra had given 90 concerts. In accord with a new municipal ordinance regarding establishment of orchestras, the Second Mayor of the Seoul Metropolitan Area named a General Director; two staffs, one for management and one for performers, were created. J.S. Kim first took the principal Conducting post, then handed the baton over in July of 1961 to Man-Bok Kim.

Tragically, many professional artists had died in action during the Korean War, and this left a dearth of soloists in the fifties and early sixties. A typical subscription concert given during that period showcased a young student, soloing in a concerto with the SPO. Today, most of these same young soloists are well-known, established professionals.

In March of 1965, the orchestra made a successful concert tour of Japan. It was the first international tour for the SPO. Then, in August of 1970, following the resignation of Man-Bok Kim, Kyung-Soo Won assumed his Post as Music Director and Conductor. In 1974, Chai-Dong Chung took the post and remains there today, steering the SPO toward its future.

Maestro Chung's appointment proved to be another turning point for the SPO. The full membership increased to 110 musicians, enabling performances of huge orchestrations by Romantics like Richard Strauss, Mahler and Bruckner. In May 1977, the orchestra went on a successful Asian tour, performing in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore. This brought the orchestra into international view.

The orchestra found a permanent home with the opening of the beautiful Seoul Sejong Cultural Center in 1978. The Seoul Metropolitan Government engaged the SPO there in July of that year. Since then the SPO has succeeded in improving both the quality as well as the variety of its concerts. Special projects include Pan-Generation Concerts, which include new music by Korean composers, Christmas Carol concerts, Pops concerts, which are a hit with audiences every year, and concerts for important national Ceremonies and holidays. Subscription concerts these days include guest conductors and soloists alike from the highest international rank. The SPO is now nearing its 380th subscription concert.

In November 1982, the orchestra toured the Western United States, stopping in Los Angeles, Santa Barbara, San Francisco and Honolulu. In May of 1986, the SPO embarked on another American tour to help publicize the Asian Games of that year and the coming Seoul Olympiad. The tour performances, in New York, Washington, D. C., Chicago, San Francisco, and Los Angeles, drew enthusiastic response from critics and packed theaters full of eager audiences.

The Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra continues to gain worldwide recognition for its fine performances, and continues to add new talent to its ranks while fostering the development of young musicians on the domestic scene. Under Maestro Chung's visionary leadership, the orchestra looks forward to an increasing number of international tours while continuing to satisfy musical appetites at home.
