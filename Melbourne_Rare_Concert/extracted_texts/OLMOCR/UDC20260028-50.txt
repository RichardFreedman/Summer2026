PART SONG—
"Indian Love Song"
The Liedertafel and Orchestra. Zollner.

ONAWAY, awake, beloved,
Thou wild flower of the forest,
Thou the wild bird of the prairie,
Thou with eyes so soft and brown!
If thou only lookest at me,
I am happy, I am happy!
As the lilies of the prairie,
When they dew upon them feel!

=== Page 5 ===
Sweet thy breath is as the fragrance of the flowers in the morn.
As their fragrance is at evening, in the Moon when leaves do fall.
Does not all the blood within me leap to meet thee, Onaway.
As the springs to meet the sunshine in the Moon when nights are bright?
    Onaway!

    Onaway! my heart sings to thee,
    Sings with joy, when thou art near,
    As the sighing, singing branches
    In the pleasant Springtime Moon.
    When thou art not pleased, beloved,
    Then my heart is sad and dark,
    As the shining river darkens
    When the clouds on it throw shade.

    When thou smilest, my beloved,
    Then my troubled heart is brightened,
    As in sunshine gleam the ripples,
    That cold winds in rivers make.

    Smiles the earth, and smile the waters,
    Smile the cloudless skies above,
    But I lose the way of smiling
    When thou art no longer near.
    Onaway! I myself, behold me!
    Blood of my beating heart, behold!
    O awake, awake, beloved,
    Onaway! beloved awake,
    Onaway! Onaway! Onaway, wake,
    Awake! loved one, Onaway!

"Departure of the Caravan"
The Liedertafel and Orchestra.

AGAIN our march we resume, going on
Across the weary wastes alone,
The depths of the desert beholding,
We forward press throughout the day,
Though days are long, and long the way,
Life no joys unfolding:
And as we go, we yearn to know
How Fate our course is moulding.
We take our way, with a song we march,
With full delight, we onward fare,
Beneath this blue overhanging arch
We freely breathe in ample air.
We sing, we march

O Sanctissima! O Piissima!
Dulcis Virgo, Maria,
Mater amata intemerata;

HAL-LOH! Hal loh! Hal-loh!
Hal-loh! o'er hill and dale we go,
Thro' smiling forest bounding,
No fear of danger can we know,
While loud the hunter's horn we blow,
The woods and craigs resounding
Echo as we go.

Tra-ra.

The sunbeams fill our hearts with glee,
With rapture now our souls are free,
All dangers then we laugh to scorn,
While merry sounds the twanging horn,
All dangers then we laugh to scorn,
While merry sounds the horn.

PART SONG—

"Happy Hours"

J. Siede.

The Liedertafel and Orchestra.

HAIL, O Night!
    Calm and bright;
    From care all hearts set free:
    Peace restoring,
    Come in thy majesty!
Shine brightly on us with thy mild beaming eye,
From thy glad presence all sorrow shall fly,
    As with the merry dance and song,
    The happy hours will pass along
        In harmony and revelry.

While pleasure reigning holds her radiant sway,
And hearts unfeigning glad homage pay,
While joy and beauty on life's pathway shine,
Why should we sorrow, why then repine?
Youth, happy youth, is life's richest prize!
Then love, then young love, beams in beauty's
    bright eyes
Banish maiden, all thoughts of sorrow,
Cares that sadden thee leave till morrow;
    And lightly as thy twinkling feet,
    Light my fond heart doth beat;
    Thy fond glance I meet,
        When thou art near.
    List to the beeze, love.
    Softly whisp'ring through the trees, love,
While the moon in her splendour sheds her rays
    from above;
Gently, so gently, on zephyrs borne, love stealing,
Softly in whispers, two fond hearts appealing;
Thine's my heart, dearest maiden,
    With love's bliss ever laden;
Thine are my thoughts, ever thine.

Hark! Hark! 'tis the midnight chime,
Wafted on the sweet wing of time;
    It sighs farewell, with parting knell.
Farewell, fair maiden, we shall meet again;
Joy's in the future though parting is pain;
Parting is brief, joy shall remain.
Fare thee well!
