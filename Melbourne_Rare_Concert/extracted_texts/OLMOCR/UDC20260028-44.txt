In Memoriam

The Society deeply deploring the sad death of the President of the United States, will sing Sir Arthur Sullivan's Part Song "THE LONG DAY CLOSES," as a tribute to the memory of a great and good man.

Part Song—The Long Day Closes
Liedertafel
SULLIVAN

No star is o'er the lake
    Its pale watch keeping;
The moon is half awake,
    Through gray mist creeping.
The last red leaves fall round
    The porch of roses,
The clock hath ceased to sound,
    The long day closes.

Sit by the silent hearth
    In calm endeavour
To count the sounds of mirth
    Now dumb for ever.
Heed not how hope believes
    And fate disposes;
Shadow is round the eaves,
    The long day closes.

The lighted windows dim
    Are fading slowly;
The fire that was so trim
    Now quivers lowly.
Go to the dreamless bed
    Where grief reposes;
Thy book of toil is read.
    The long day closes.

Programme

PART I

Grand Double Chorus from Oedipus at Colonos
MUSIC BY FELIX MENDELSSOHN-BARTHOLDY

Liedertafel

THOU comest here to the land, O friend.
Fam'd for fleet-footed steeds and blooming meadows:
Thou standest now in Colonos' grove,
    Where the voices of nightingales
    Resound, floating in dulcet strains
        Thro' their temples of verdure;
    Where the thick-woven ivy clings,
    Spreading over the sacred aisles;
Where, in security, ripens the fruitful vine.
    Unscath'd by beam or stormwind.
And here roameth the bounteous god.
Gay and good Dionysos, ever greeted in lays,
    Sung by the nymphs who rear'd him.

    Fair and fragrant at dawn of day,
    See the beautiful heaven-bedewed Narcissus.
The flow'r that shone in the goddesses' crowns,
    Worn by them in the days of old.

=== Page 9 ===
See the Crocus array'd in gold;
    Hear the slumberless waters,
While they glide in Cephisus' tide,
Still meandering day by day
Over the plains, bearing streams ever pure along,
    Enriching all the bowers
Embosomed in the favour'd land;
Ever loved by the tuneful choir of Muses,
    And Cythera, whose zone is golden.

Here, too, bloometh a tree
Crowned with a fruit not found in Asia:
Nor on Dorian plains doth it appear.
Gracing the island where the Pelops
Held sway: yet here it freely springeth:
    Rever'd by friends, and fear'd by foes.
    In native splendour it blossoms for Athens.
Behold, ours is the rich oil-dropping olive;
    Aged and youthful, noble and peasant,
Never wantonly harm this holy tree,
    Guarded and cherished, it stands secure.
    Ever heeded by Morian Jove,
    Lov'd and watch'd by Minerva.

Grateful, let me extol
Heaven's richest gifts deigned to my country:
Presented by the Pow'r making our State
Fam'd for its wealth, honour, and glory:
    Our land and sea steeds, rein and rudder-guided:
    O Chronos' son, by them we stand pre-eminent.
        Mighty god Poseidon,
Thou didst give us the rein formed for the courser;
Guided thus, or curbed, he yields obedience.
Through the billows we dash, stemming the deep,
    Speeded along by the oar and helm,
    Bounding on with the dancing nymphs—
        Nereus' hundred-footed daughters.

Part Song—The Better Land
(Composed expressly for the Society)
JOSEPH KENDALL

Liedertafel

I hear thee speak of the Better Land,
Thou call'st its children a happy band;
Mother, where is that radiant shore?
Shall we not seek it and weep no more?
Is it where the flow'r of the orange blows,
And the fireflies dance thro' the myrtle boughs?
    Not there, not there, my child.

Is it far away in some region old,
Where the rivers wander o'er sands of gold,
Where the burning rays of the ruby shine,
And the diamond lights up the secret mine,
And the pearls gleam forth from the coral strand?
Is it there, sweet mother, that better land?
    Not there, not there, my child.

Eye hath not seen it, my gentle boy;
Ear hath not heard its sweet songs of joy;
Dreams cannot picture a world so fair:
Sorrow and death may not enter there;
Time doth not breathe on its fadeless bloom:
For beyond the clouds, beyond the tomb.
    It is there, my child, it is there.

Part Song—The Retreat
Liedertafel
L. de Rille

HARK! the tattoo the hour is beating,
    The sound obey;
Come, troopers, come away.
Disgrace on him will fall
Who disregards the call.
To quarters now retreating,
    Come, troopers, come away,
    The sound obey.
Shadows of darkness o'er us hover,
No friendly form can we discover.
    Night reigns around,
Loud rolls the drum, the horns replying,
And echo's voice in distance dying
    Repeats the sound.
Yon fortress tow'ring,
With an aspect low'ring,
Obscure as night,
Conceals from sight
Each roof and dwelling height.
While all are sleeping
Our watch we are keeping.
With sword and lance
And eagle glance
The ready troops advance.

Song—O Murm'ring Breezes
Mr. Gregor Wood

Morning in the Forest
Liedertafel

O SUMMER night serenely still!
No sound the forest haunting,
In hollow glade alone, the rill
Its song is ever chanting.
The flow'rs are nodding with sleep-laden eyes.
A dream of dawn on the branches lies,
And peace on valley and hill.

Hark! 'tis the lark's thrilling note on high
The forest's silence breaking:
Ye sleepers, arise, in the Orient sky
The rosy morn is awaking.
Trill on, sweet herald, in jubilant lay,
Welcome the kiss of the joyous day.
As westward the shadows hie.

Now sweetly faint the distance o'er
The matin chimes are stealing,
And soon within the bosom's core
The joy bells all are pealing.
Adoring sound, exultant song,
It rings and sings the world along:
Praise God for evermore!

Tenor Solo and Chorus—
Hymn to the Almighty
Mr. Gregor Wood and Liedertafel

Schubert-Liszt

Great is Jehovah the Lord!
The heavens and the earth both proclaim His mighty power.
We hear Him when roareth the storm,
When the wind through the woods rushes along.
    Great is Jehovah the Lord!
    Mighty is His power.

Thou hear'st it in the green budding boughs of the forest.
Seest it in the cornfields that glow like gold,
In flowers ye may see it in bright varied hues,
And in the stars still His marvellous power behold.
We see Him in heaven's high arches of starry splendour.

Awful rolls He in thunder on high,
The flame of His lightning darts in terror below,
And each beating heart, every soul feels His wondrous power and might.
We bow to the power dwelling in Him,
The Lord of all, and God everlasting.

Thou to Him look for aid,
In hope to gain His love and grace.
Turning then, we implore His love and grace and protection.
    Great is Jehovah the Lord!

Part Song—The Tyrol
Liedertafel
AMBROISE THOMAS

Poom, poom, poom! home!
The distant bell is pealing,
Down the mountains slowly stealing
    The morning sunbeams play.
When God, his hand revealing,
    Beckons darkness away,
In songs of grateful feeling.
We hail the rising day.
    Oh! behold the day.

We sing of Tyrol, her mountains and waters,
    The gloomy ravine, the wild shaggy wood;
Her true-hearted sons, her fair blooming daughters,
    The valiant and free, the gentle and good.
And chiefly we sing that sweet, simple strain,
Which echo returns from yon rocks again.
Dear strains of Tyrol, how sweet to the soul;
    Gentle song, float along.

    Ah, ah! la la la la! la la la la!
Oh! once we were fond of carelessly saying,
    Our home was the world in which we would roam,
And half round the world we might have gone straying,
    And never have found for our hearts a home.

=== Page 15 ===
Till passing one day thro' the mountains near,
    Sweet echo let fail this strain on our ear:
Dear strains of Tyrol, how sweet to the soul;
    Gentle song, float along.

    Ah! ah! Hoo! hoo!
What dismal sounds are crashing athwart the air?
See lightning flashing everywhere.
    It is the thunderstorm!
    How the wind roars!
    How the rain pours!
    Hark how loud the thunders crash!
    The wind doth roar,
    The rain doth pour,
    Ah, that fearful crash!
    The storm winds shatter the pines around.
'Tis the hurricane:
Hailstones clatter upon the ground.
Flee away, mountaineers!
The lightning dazzles every eye.
It flashes nigher and more nigh:
Ah, the hailstones lash the flying mountaineer
The rain torrents pour.
Ah! how it doth roar;
The storm wind doth roar;
Ha, but the thunder farther away doth run,
Clouds rent asunder, out shines the sun!
Sound again, tender strain, sound again,
    We'll sing la la la la la'!

Gaily dancing,
Swiftly glancing,
Tripping lightly,
Now skipping sprightly,
Tread we now a gay mincing measure,
Dance and song will sweeten our leisure;
Softly treading, nimbly threading,
In and out the merry rout.

=== Page 16 ===
Singing, dancing, take we our pleasure,
Pray, keep step, beat the time to the merry rout.
'Tis of Tyrol we sing, this gentle strain we sing.
Once again, gentle strain. 'tis lovely Tyrol
    We sing, la la la!

Banditti Chorus from "Ernani"  VERDI
Liedertafel
All welcome the bowl!
A full flowing soul,
    Unfathomed by care!
What boots it the bandit,
If hunted and branded,
    So wine be his share.

    To play, sirs!
Treasure is merely a pleasure
    That flies soon as come!
And play hath a duty,
It charmeth when beauty
    Inconstant may roam.

=== Page 17 ===
Thrice welcome the bowl!
Whose glorious soul
    Doth bleed at our call!
The bowl, the bowl,
Whose glorious souls, sirs,
    Doth bleed at our call.

Hernani, still pensive!
Quick thought grows apprehensive
    To view his cheek so blanch'd.
Where life's tide hath motion,
On Death's shoreless ocean
    Our one bark is launch'd.

To give, then, thy willing
Immediate fulfilling,
    Ere will'd we prepare;
The hawk's speed e'en tarries
In hitting her quarries,
    If ours ye compare.

Come, welcome the bowl.
    Whose glorious soul
Can drown all thy care!

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced, and the entrance fee abolished.

This is the fifth Concert of the Twenty-eighth Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only.

Intending subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s.; Class C, £1 11s. 6d.

Class A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 tickets.

Class B.—Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 tickets.

Class C.—Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 tickets.

Life Subscribers.—The sum of Ten Guineas will secure for life the privileges of Class A.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.
