Part Song

"Champagnerlied"
(Sele—Mr. JAMES WADE).
Heinrich Hofmann

THE LIEDERTAFEL.

Pour the libation in fizzing champagne,
Leaping in silvery, swirling cascades;
Pour the libation in fizzing champagne,
Gushing in tremulous, prancing, proud courses.
Flowing in boiling and high foaming ardours,
Like to the olden-time streamlets and sources
Deeply in Ida's dun thickets concealed.

Mould there thy pearl drops from silvery foam;
Seething they climb from their specular home.
Mould there thy pearl drops from silvery foam.
There are the sharp infundibular goblets,
Sparkling and glistening like bubbling fountains,
Pour'd from the mouth of the broken sealed magnum
Deeply in Ida's dun thickets concealed.

Swaying and thinking in aureate room,
Clink now the glasses with resonant swing;
Rolling and swaying like zephyrs in spring-time.
Dance frolicsome genii of champagne;
Backward and forward enlacing departing,
Glistening, vanishing, lost in the crystal vase;
Brief as the day of youth's gay effervescence.

Part Song—
"Serenade"
Plumpton

THE LIEDERTAFEL.

If thou art sleeping maiden,
  Awake, awake and open thy door,
'Tis the break of day and we must away
  O'er meadow and mount and moor.
Wait not to find the slippered feet,
  But come with thy silken feet,
We shall have to pass through the dewy grass
  And the waters wide and fleet.

THE LIEDERTAFEL.

The Nation's glory into your hands is now given.
  Oh, guard it well.
To sink with you, or with you rise to heaven,
  The magic spell of heart and song
    Links all the worlds that roll along,
So may they ever blended be in richest harmony.
Her voice is like the dreadful thunder,
  Terrible in its might and anger;
It rises on victorious planks and vengeance
  Takes on foes and minions—
    Strikes terror to the tyrant's heart.
If truth to exile e'er be driven;
Then to her aid the Muse arisen
Shall find revenge in lofty tuneful song.
Oh, freedom's soul of light arise!
Lift up your head to beauty's throne,
And as with her you mount the skies,
All lesser crowns disown.
The thousand various ways unending
Of rich humanity explore,
Till from the summit's height extending,
Behold the vast eternal shore.

"Lend me your aid"
MR. JAMES WADE.

Part Song —
"Thou art my dream"
THE LIEDERTAFEL.

THE drooping flowers their buds are closing.
The silver stars keep watch above:
The world in tranquil sleep reposing
My wakeful bosom yields to love.
While here in hopeless grief I languish,
Could I behold thy vision bright,
Then would I fondly breathe my anguish,
Thou art my dream thro' stilly night.

Tho' all unmoved by love's appealing,
No kindling passion lights thy brow?
No voice of love thy heart revealing,
Still, lady, still, list to my song.
Bend softly down, ye golden tresses,
Beam kindly once, bright orbs of light:
List, lady, list to love's distresses—
Thou art my dream thro' stilly night.

(a) "Zur Guitarre"

Part Song —
"Die Post"
(Cornet Solo—Mr. ARTHUR HAIGH).
THE LIEDERTAFEL.

THRO' forest deep at dead of night,
The mail is slowly borne;
While sleep the weary travellers,
The post-boy winds his horn.

The forest-ranger's dwelling
Within the wood is seen,
The horn a tale is telling—
What can its echoes mean?

Its tunes so soft and sweetly flow,
The mails, in passing by,
Pause at the open window,
And mingling with them fly.

Where sleeps the forest maiden,
While beams the moon above:
With love her dreams are laden—
With melody and love.

Aria —
"Elsa's Dream"
(Lohengrin)
MISS MARGUERITE HENDERSON.

Part Song—
"Song of the Pirates"
Bruch

THE LIEDERTAFEL.

On land we find but scanty honour,
A vagrant folk without a home;
Exiles to-day on shore we wonder,
To-morrow monarchs of the foam!
    Clink again!
    Glasses drain!
    Drink amain!

Our coats are good broad-cloth from England,
And heavy silk from Genoa;
Our scores we reckon with the grapnel,
And we receipt them with "Hurrah!"
    Clink again!
    Glasses drain!
    Drink amain!

When o'er the deep our sails are flying,
All things must bow to our control;
On every head a tax we levy
And ocean's pride must pay us toll.
    Clink again!
    Glasses drain!
    Drink amain!

Here's glorious beer! That comes from Hamburg!
Or is your taste for Spanish wine?
All climes are pledged to feed and clothe us,
All fill the cup when we would dine.
    Clink again!
    Glasses drain!
    Drink amain!

Gay go the hours with wine and singing,
A merry life beyond compare!
And when at last our skulls lay bleaching,
Why there's an end to further care.
    Clink again!
    Glasses drain!
    Drink amain!
