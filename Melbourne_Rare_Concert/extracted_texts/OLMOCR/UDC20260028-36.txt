Part Song—On the Mountains

Liedertafel

Oh! How bright the mountains rise,
Grand and glorious to the skies.
Where the peaks are white with snow,
Where the alpine roses grow;
Where the horn gives out a strain,
That in echo comes again.

Oh! How bright the mountains rise,
Grand and glorious to the skies.
Where is heard the sheep-bell's sound,
Where the herds are grazing round;
Where the torrent's noisy flow
Pours in thunder far below.

Oh! How bright the mountains rise,
Grand and glorious to the skies.
Where the heart with joy can glow,
Leaving pain and care below;
Where the eye its glance can raise,
And on Heaven thankful gaze

Part Song--Evening Rest
Liedertafel
Hamma

The sun has now descended,
The woodland notes are ended.
Soft in the swaying trees
Sighs now the evening breeze,
While from afar yet hover
The sounds of day, scarce over,
And all to rest incline.
O heart, what rest is thine?

On heav'n's broad arch encroaching,
The night is fast approaching,
And in the vault above
Shines now the star of love.
And nightingales are singing,
And onward softly winging,
For love the spirit yearns,
And restless, homeward turns.

Part Song—Hymn to the Night
(Baritone Solo—Mr. Henry Rofe.)
Möhrin

Liedertafel

I heard the trailing garments of the night
Sweep through her marble halls!
I saw her sable skirts all fringed with light
From the celestial walls!

I felt her presence, by its spell of might,
Stoop o’er me from above:
The calm majestic presence of the night,
As of the one I love.

I hear the sounds of sorrow and delight
The manifold soft chimes,
That fill the haunted chambers of the night,
Like some old poet’s rhymes.

=== Page 10 ===
From the cool cistern of the midnight air
    My spirit drank repose;
The fountain of perpetual peace flows there,
    From those deep cisterns flows.

O holy night! from thee I learn to bear
    What man has borne before!
Thou layest thy finger on the lips of care,
    And they complain no more.

Peace! peace! Orestes-like I breathe this prayer.
    Descend with broad-winged flight,
The welcome, the thrice prayed for, the most fair,
    The best-beloved night!

PART II

Cantata – The Waterlily

Miss Maggie Stirling and Liedertafel

In evening stillness lies the lake,
    And mirrors the waning glow of the west,
Their thirst come timorous deer to slake,
    And swans fly, encircling its tranquil breast.

The fisher boy unfastens his boat,
    The plash of his oar thro' the silence steals:
In Fancy's realm does he appear to float,
    He knows not why in his heart thus he feels.

Around him whisper the darkling waves,
    And sing in their motion, far and wide,
The water a dim wooded margin laves,
    And the spectre-like mountains tower in pride.

The cooling breath of the evening air,
    It plays thro' his locks all golden and bright,
A fragrance comes from the flow'rets fair,
    As low they are bending for sleep at night.

But the waterlily is yet awake,
    Though nodding in slumb'rous mood is she;
She bends unto him from her native lake,
    Tho' evening invites her at rest to be.

=== Page 12 ===
His dream is dispelled, and he quits his oar;
    His face is on fire with longing confessed;
He stoops from the boat, the spot to explore,
    He plucks off a lily, thus decking his breast.

But hark! with fury the forest shakes,
    The lake uplifts its roaring on high,
And sudden over a mountain breaks
    The ruddy full moon, so wrathful of eye.

And the lake exclaims, with heart deeply moved,
    "Why trouble the children within their home?"
And the forest complains, "These long have I loved,
    Why force these delightful sisters to roam?"

But the red-glowing moon then fiercely cries,
    "How darest thou to address my bride?"
And in terror the boy with tears homeward flies,
    That there, with his mother, his grief he may hide.

    "O mother, the roar of the lake I heard,
    Against me the forest with fury shook,
Before me, in anger, the full moon appeared,
    O mother, I but a lily took!"

Part Song—Prayer before Battle
Liedertafel
STORCH

Hear us, Almighty One!
Hear us, All Holy one!
    Dark rolls the battle before us.
Father, all praise to Thee!
Father, all thanks to Thee!
    That freedom's banner is o'er us!

Like a consuming brand,
Stretch forth Thy mighty hand,
    Over oppression victorious.
Help us maintain the right,
Help us, oh, God of Might.
    Help us, Thy cause must be glorious.

Help us! though we may fall,
From out the grave we call
Praise to Thy mercy for ever.
All pow'r and glory be Thine
Thro' eternity. Amen! Amen! Amen!

Part Song—
On the Sparkling Snow  AMBROISE THOMAS

Liedertafel

On the sparkling snow so lightly we go,
    While the moon's full glow you cloud is dividing.
To the whip's cheerful crack the bells tinkle back
    As gaily we go on the snow swiftly gliding.
So light we go, on th' sparkling snow.
In the winter night, by the pale moon-light,
The fancy hath leisure to follow her pleasure.

    Good steeds gallop on, with the wind we fly,
        As it wanders by with a whisper'd sigh!

Care begone, you are no great treasure,
You spoil every pleasure.  Crick, crack!  Hurrah!
With the wind we fly, as it wanders by
With a whispered sigh, gallop on!
Never look behind!  Hurrah!  &c.

Happy hearts we bring, gay laughter shall ring,
Tho' the fleet hoofs fling snow-dust in our faces.
    And swift as we fly, our fortune we try,
And just for the fun, we will run friendly races.
    So swift we fly, we'll pass you by,
In the winter breeze, while the leafless trees,
Now shake their clear shadows o'er frost-bound white meadows.

    Good steeds gallop on, &c.

Care good-bye, for now we must part,
Don't fancy the loss will break my heart.
    Crick, crack!  Hurrah!  &c.

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced.

This is the third Concert of the Twenty-third Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only.

Intending Subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s.; Class C, £1 11s. 6d. Entrance fee to each Class, 10s. 6d.

Class A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 TICKETS.

Class B.—Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 TICKETS.

Class C.—Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 TICKETS.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.
