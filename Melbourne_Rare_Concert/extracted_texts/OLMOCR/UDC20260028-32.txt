PART I

Battle of the Baltic
A Choral Ballad for Male Chorus and Baritone Solo
BY J. ALBERT MALLINSON

Mr. Frederick Ferguson and Liedertafel

Of Nelson and the North
Sing the glorious day's renown,
When to battle fierce went forth
All the might of Denmark's crown,
And her arms along the deep proudly shone.
By each gun the lighted brand
In a bold determined hand;
And the prince of all the land
    Led them on.

Like leviathans afloat
Lay their bulwarks on the brine;
While the sign of battle flew
On the lofty British line.
It was ten of April morn by the chime;
As they drifted on their path,
There was silence deep as death,
And the boldest held his breath
    For a time.

=== Page 8 ===
But the might of England flushed
To anticipate the scene;
And her van the fleeter rushed
O'er the deadly space between.
"Hearts of oak!" our captains cried, when each gun
From its adamantine lips
Spread a death shade round the ships,
Like the hurricane eclipse
    Of the sun.

Again! again! again!
And the havoc did not slack,
Till a feeble cheer the Dane
To our cheering sent us back;
Their shots along the deep slowly boom—
Then ceased—and all is wail,!
As they strike the shattered sail,
Or, in conflagration pale,
    Light the gloom.

Then cried the victor,
As he hailed them o'er the wave:—
"Ye are brothers! ye are men!
And we conquer but to save:—
So peace instead of death let us bring:
But yield, proud foe, thy fleet,
With the crews, at England's feet,
And make submission meet
    To our King."

Now joy, old England, raise!;
For the tidings of thy might,
By the festal cities' blaze,
While the wine cup shines in light;
And yet amidst that joy and uproar,
Let us think of them that sleep,
Full many a fathom deep,
By thy wild and stormy steep,
    Elsinore!

=== Page 9 ===
Brave hearts! to Britain's pride
Once so faithful and so true,
On the deck of fame that died,--
With the gallant good Riou:
Soft sigh the winds of heaven o'er their grave
While the billow mournful rolls,
And the mermaid's song consoles,
Singing glory to the souls
    Of the brave.

Where waves the forest far around,

O'er forest, meadow, stream, and crag,

To sing a soft good night.

My heart is thine for ever,
Thou art the hunter's joy.

PART II

Grand Double Chorus—Ah! were I on Yonder Plain (Antigone) MENDELSsohn

Liedertafel

Ah, were I on yonder plain,
Where now our undaunted sons
Stand armed for the hostile fray!
There where the shrine of Phoebus gleams,
And torches are burning;
Where mortals bow down to the awe-inspiring pair,
Whose hallowed rites are mystic deeds,
Wrought by Eumolpidan priests,
And never breathed to mortal ear!
There, glowing with ardour,
Panting to rush against the foe,
See all our legions!
With Theseus they wave their swords, and cry,
Rescue the sisters!
Or westward perchance they rush,
Where snow-covered mountains rise
O'er Œa's enamelled meads;
There dancing cars and prancing steeds
Will clash in the onset!
There on the foe soon will impending vengeance fall;
For Theseus leads our valiant bands.
See their arms and armour gleaming,
Bright as the trappings that grace their steeds;
Now proudly exulting,
They who honour the deeds of sage Minerva,
And Rhea's great son, earth-encircling ocean's king,
Press to the battle!

=== Page 12 ===
Do they await, pursue, or fight?
    My heart thrills with hope
That they will soon rescue the captive maid,
Who hath endured and still endures her kindred's woe.
    This day Jove will restore her;
    Crown your deeds, heroes, with glory!
    Like a dove high soaring,
    Sped along by the storm-wind,
    I, from the verge of a cloud,
Would look down on the contest,
And see my country's cause triumphant!

Dread Power, that fillest heaven's high throne,
O all-seeing Jove, defend our great ruler!
    On him the victor's palm bestow,
    With verdant laurels deck his brow!
        We call on thee, Pallas Athene;
        Mighty hunter, Phoebus, hear us!
And thy Sister, fleet in chase, whose dart unerring
Pierceth the far-distant hart.
    Oh come, defend our guardians!
    We pray ye, aid our native heroes!

Vocal March —
The Tyrolese Hunters' March
Liedertafel and Orchestra
KOSCHAT

HALLO! Hallo!
Now hunters come away, for early breaks the day;
With rifles prov'd and tried we range the mountain side.
Keep distance every man, each nook and covert scan;
From by the forest end to where the two ways blend.

Yoho, yoho, yoho! There something crouches low;
What eyes can shine like that? It is a mountain cat.
'Tis only so I fear: It is a crouching deer;
Surround it every way, before it steals away.

See now he springs and flies, and for the forest tries,
But there the hunters stand, with steady eye and hand.
And crack it goes, yoho! the ball has laid him low;
A noble beast is he, and killed most manfully.

Here from the waterfall we send a hunter's cheer,
To glad the list'ning ears of wives and sweethearts dear.
So gently timid they, that if we shoot they say—
"Ah me! there's someone hurt to-day."

O sweethearts in the vale, you from afar we hail,
With joyful notes that tell that all with us is well.
Yet here awhile we roam: and with the evening come
To seek our dear and quiet home.

Tra la, la la, la la! then there's hurrah, hurrah!
For such a bonny land, for vales and mountains grand.
And here's hurrah, hurrah! for wives and maidens true.
Sweethearts, we shout for you, hurrah!

An eye that never fails, a heart that never quails,
A cheek aglow with health, is more than idle wealth.
Hallo! hallo, hallo! a-hunting we will go;
By topmost crag and peak our timid prey we seek.
Hallo! hallo, hallo! a-hunting we will go;
Full quickly flies the day: Hallo, hallo, away!

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced, and the entrance fee abolished.

This is the first Concert of the Twenty-fourth Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only.

Intending Subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s., Class C, £1 11s. 6d.

Class A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 TICKETS.

Class B. Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 TICKETS.

Class C. Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 TICKETS.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.
