"FRITHJOF"

The story of Frithjof the Bold and Ingeborg the Fair is told in "Frithjof's Saga," a poem in twenty-four cantos, by Esaias Tegner, published in 1820.

The hero, Frithjof, is son to Thorsten Vikingsson, who, though King Bele's best warrior and chosen friend, is of lowly origin. Frithjof the Strong and Bold dares to love Fair Ingeborg, daughter of his king and descendant of great Odin. Bele looks kindly on young Frithjof's love, but death takes from the youth at once a monarch and a friend. When Frithjof subsequently demands the hand of Ingeborg from Helge, son and successor of Bele, he is rejected with scorn. Later old King Ring, a mighty monarch of the North, would make fair Ingeborg his queen, but Helge, influenced by soothsayers, sends a refusal, accompanied by a taunting message from brother Halfdan to "Old Grey-beard." King Ring, incensed, declares war against the brothers, and Ingeborg is sent for safety to the vale of Baldur, the god of innocence and virtue. While in the sacred grove the maiden receives a visit from her lover, the joy of which is followed by the fear that she has angered the gods by keeping a love-tryst in that holy place. At a council of war, assembled at Bele's cairn, Frithjof again demands from Helge the hand of Ingeborg, in return for which he offers his best aid in the impending battle. Helge again repels him, and forces him to make the fatal admission that he has visited Ingeborg in the sacred grove. He thereupon denounces him as a profaner of temples, and orders him to do penance for his sacrilege by sailing to the Orkneys to wring from fierce Earl Agantyr long-withheld tribute. Frithjof tries to persuade Ingeborg to fly with him, but in vain. Giving her his arm-ring, he departs, promising to return and make her his bride.

Borne through his perilous voyage by his charmed ship "Elida," Frithjof returns at length to his native land to find his estates laid waste with fire and sword, and the fair Ingeborg given in marriage to victorious King Ring. He learns that at the marriage ceremony the cowardly Helge tore from his sister's arm her lover's ring, and hung it upon the arm of Baldur's image. Frithjof, furious, seeks out the King whilst he is in the act of offering sacrifice at Baldur's pyre. He flings Agantyr's tribute full in Helge's face, and, hastily snatching at the arm-ring, he overturns the huge idol upon the sacred fire. The flames lap it about, and, leaping upward with angry roar, spread to the temple and the sacred grove, which are soon reduced to ashes.

Frithjof is now shunned by all as a perpetrator of sacrilege; he is banished from his country, and, sailing away in the "Elida," he devotes himself to a life of wild adventure. The hero's troubled life is ultimately crowned with happiness. After many years he returns to his native land, and succeeds in gaining the goodwill of King Ring, who at his death bequeaths him his kingdom and his queen.

INTRODUCTION

Scene I.
Frithjof's Return

Recitative.

How bravely o'er the floods so bright,
Ellida flies to shore now in sight!
Although the king with demon spell
Invoked the horrid goblin brood,
And unloosed the wings of the tempest,
My Northland courage bore me
Through ardent strife
To glory's height;
From mighty Jarl the tribute I obtained:
I greet thee gladly, Baldur's grove,
Where oft by break of night
Hope filled my heart with sweet delight!
My Ingeborg, but where art thou now?
Beloved maiden, art thou true to me?

See, I vie with the winds and their heavenly train.
Ellida, O hasten and fly o'er the main.

Chorus.

Oh, 'tis delight when the land afar appeareth,
And from the hearth of thy home beloved
The smoke curls upwards
And thou art near to childhood's scenes
For ever dear.

Solo—Frithjof.

On the tomb of my father I planted the lime-tree,
Is the delicate stem still alive?
Give, earth, thy growth,
And thou O heaven, send fostering dew.
Oh, 'tis delight!

Chorus of Priests.
Midnight sun on the mountain burns,
    Red like blood appearing,

Go to Hela's dark abode.

Frithjof.
Hush, wretched priests with gory knives.
    Moonlight's pallid princes!

    Long our blades have thirsted!
Where my father rests,
Where my childhood passed,
Nought save a ruin I found aghast!

Scene IV.
Frithjof Goes Into Exile

Solo—Quartette
Sun in the sky
Now mounteth high.
The winds are blowing
And gently flowing,
The waves advance
In joyful dance.

Chorus.
Sadly Frithjof goes his way;
See, he weepeth, while morning gloweth!

Solo—Frithjof and Chorus.
World's grandest region thou mighty North,
From thy dominions I'm driven forth.
Within thy borders I lov'd to dwell!
Midsummer sun, fare thee well!
My love is foiled, my rooftree rent!
With honour soiled, in exile sent!
Cheerless is my soul within me.
Hopeless, I must bear my lot.
Ye rugged mountains where heroes dwell,
And Thor commandeth clouds and winds:
Ye azure lakes that I love so well.
Ye woods and brakes, fare ye well!

Scene V.
Ingeborg's Lament

Solo—Ingeborg.
Storms wildly roar!
Billows break madly upon the shore.
Would 'neath the sod I were lying.
Winds o'er me sighing!
Sad and oppress.
Thee I did watch, flying sail in the west!
Onward with Frithjof thou glidest,
With him abidest.
Falcon, beloved! thee he left here;
Falcon for his sake to me thou art dear.
Shake not thy pinions, stay near me,
O, why should'st thou fear me?
Here on his hand,
Worker on the canvas, thou proudly shall stand,
Silver thy pinions unfolden;
Thy claws shall be golden.
Thou darling bird!
Sit on my shoulder, and list to my word!
When I'm no more
He will come hither, and his heart will be sore:
Greet from me Frithjof.
Who loves me, and never forgets me.

Scene VI.
Frithjof On the Sea

Chorus—Frithjof's Followers.
Now he crossed the floods of the salt desert waste.
Far off like a falcon he flew!
But for warriors on board he wrote maxims and rules!
List to the laws of his crew!

Frithjof and Chorus.
On this deck raise no tent,
And ashore do not sleep:
Within houses the foe lurks for thee!

On thy shield sleep, Viking.
Thy sword in thy hand:
Let the heavens thy canopy be.
Now hold high your lances—
Your lances hold high—
Our banners unfold ye,
The triumph is nigh.
To climes of the south we shall wander.
When hurricanes rage,
Hoist the canvas on high!
Let them come as they may:
He who flinches must die.
Rise, rise, unfold ye our banners!

(a) Holy night, oh soothe my breast
With thy solemn tranquil calm:
Give the weary wand'rer rest,
O'er his sorrows shed thy balm.
Stars brightly o'er me gleaming,
From azure fields are beaming,
They set my soul a-dreaming
Of rest above.
Hallowed sounds fall on my ear,
Wafted on the nightwind's sighs:
Murmurings from some blissful sphere,
Echoes from love's paradise.
On high my glances turning,
Where heaven's stars are burning,
I dream with fervent yearning
Of peace and love.

O'er the meadow and mount and moor.
Wait not to find thy slippers,

We shall have to pass thro' the dewy grass

All welcome the bowl!

Thrice welcome the bowl!
Whose glorious soul
Doth bleed at our call!
The bowl, the bowl,
Whose glorious soul, sirs,
Doth bleed at our call.

Hernani, still pensive?
Quick thought grows apprehensive
To view his cheek so 'blanch'd
Where's life's tide hath motion.
On Death's shoreless ocean
Our one bark is launched.
To give, then, thy willing
Immediate fulfilling.
Ere will'd we prepare.
The hawk's speed 'e'en tarries
In hitting her quarries
If ours ye compare.
Come, welcome the bowl.
Whose glorious soul
Can drown all thy care!
