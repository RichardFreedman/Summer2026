PART I

Part Song—St. John's Eve        Rheinberger
Liedertafel

How mild the balmy summer night!
    Upon the heights what fires are glowing!
All is around us fragrant, bright,
    The boatmen chant below us rowing.
The azure heav'n above is beaming,
    Now rests the world, the waters glide,
    A wond'rous dome outstretching wide,
And earth its broad foundation seeming.

Oh, summer night! in holy light,
    With perfume, light, and grace o'erflowing,
With stars like eyes of angels bright,
    Their placid smile on all bestowing.
There floats a chord of love along,
    And earth with heav'n is thus united,
And silenced too are sin and wrong,
    As love alone could here be plighted.

Oh, therefore lighted is the blaze
    Upon the heights, and fire is gleaming;
Oh, therefore all the planets gaze,
    And earth and heav'n alike are beaming.
Now rests the world, and as the wind
    Now in the tree-tops gently rustleth,
Such peace and beauty are combined,
    That man rejoiceth, prayeth, list'neth.

Part Song—The Hamlet SCHUBERT
(Translated from the German by Mr. H. W. PÜTTMANN.)
Liedertafel

I love to vaunt
My rustic haunt:
For fairer graces
Than all around
The eye embraces
Nowhere are found.
Here spreads the glory
Of ripening corn,
There woodlands hoary
The scene adorn;
The white flocks daily
On yonder lea,
And in the valley
Stands "Sorrow-free."
For thus my hamlet
I love to greet,
My hermitage,
My blest retreat:
Where morn comes bracing
To cheerful task,
And, interlacing,
The vine and elmtree
The noonday mask.

=== Page 9 ===
The wild sloes' tangle
Defines yon ravine,
The poplars spangle
With silvery sheen;
And softly rustling
By fernshaded nook,
Now idling, now bustling,
Meanders the brook:
Through furtive shadows
Of arching boughs,
And sun-gilt meadows
Serenely it flows;
Then lucently glasses
The green hills it passes,
Where lambkins stray;
It mirrors the flowers,
And cool in its bowers
The groundlings play.
See, darting and gliding,
The clear flood dividing,
The roach and trout:
Now downward feigning,
Then swiftly about,
The surface regaining.
    O happy state!
May Time and Fate
Undo thee never;
To strength renewed
And joyous mood
Restore me ever!

Part Song—
A Message to Phyllis
Liedertafel

Ye little birds that sit and sing
    Amidst the shady valleys,
And see how Phyllis sweetly walks
    Within her garden alleys;
Go, pretty birds, about her bower;
Sing, pretty birds, she may not lower;
Ah me! methinks I see her frown!
    Ye pretty wantons, warble.

Go, tell her through your chirping bills,
    As you by me are bidden,
To her is only known my love,
    Which from the world is hidden.
Go, pretty birds, and tell her so;
See that your notes strain not too low,
For still, methinks, I see her frown!
    Ye pretty wantons, warble.

Go, tune your voices' harmony,
    And sing, I am her lover;
Strain loud and sweet, that every note
    With sweet content may move her.
And she that hath the sweetest voice,
Tell her I will not change my choice,
Yet still, methinks, I see her frown!
    Ye pretty wantons, warble.

Oh fly! make haste! see, see, she falls
    Into a pretty slumber.
Sing round about her rosy bed,
    That, waking, she may wonder.
Say to her, 'tis her lover true
That sendeth love to you, to you;
And when you hear her kind reply,
    Return with pleasant warblings.

PART II

Part Song—The Nightingale
Schubert
(Translated from the German by Mr. H. W. Püttmann.)

Liedertafel

CONCEALED in the leafy recess of a grove
Sweet Philomel warbled the beauty of love,
She sang of its rapture, its longing and pain
In jubilant cadence, in thrilling refrain.

Her own bosom's yearning gave voice to her throat,
Each tone spoke of passion, a sigh was each note;
Then died into silence at last, like the bliss
Of true hearts in union grown mute in a kiss.

Thus often, O friends, have our souls been regaled,
When Cynthia's lamp o'er the darkness prevailed,
When ecstasy came on the pinions of song,
And love in our hearts linger'd gladly and long.

Part Song—Home
(The Words by Mr. H. W. Püttmann.)
FISCHER
Liedertafel

The winds rush onward, the clouds they drift,
But question them never: "Say whither so swift?"
The clouds and the winds ne'er an answer give they,
But joyless and painless they drift on their way.

The brooklets and rivers flow murm'ring along,
But question them never: "What meaneth your song?"
They ripple and murmur their self-same refrain,
Thou waitest unheeded, and askest in vain.

The wild swans fly southward when summer is o'er,
But question them never: "Return ye no more?"
They follow the summer, they reckon not where,
Their home is the wide world, be summer but there.

But thou, my heart, look'st back with a sigh!
O question it never: "For why, ah why?"
Who never has felt it, will ne'er understand,
My heart turns to thee still, beloved native land.

Part Song—The Lifeboat
Liedertafel
HATTON

'Tis night! upon the Cornish coast full loud the breakers roar,
And helplessly yon gallant bark drifts on the dark lee shore,
And quickly now the signal guns boom high above the gale,
Oh! many a dark-eyed Cornish girl at that wild sound grows pale.

The life-boat's mann'd, stand clear ahead, there's death upon the gale,
Cheer up, dear lass, one parting kiss, your lips look cold and pale.
The life-boat's mann'd, stand clear ahead, no time to sigh for home,
Hurrah! the gallant life-boat sweeps thro' the seething foam.

=== Page 13 ===
Bend boldly to your task, brave hearts, it is a glorious strife,
On ev'ry oar-blade flashing high, there hangs some loved one's life.
A cheer so faint comes down the wind, all hands we yet may save,
Now lift our gallant life-boat like lightning o'er the wave.

Part Song—Loyal Song Kücken
Liedertafel
FREEDOM dwells thro'out our own beloved land:
    Up to heav'n its voice is swelling.
From the mountain heights afar to ocean strand
    Ev'ry breeze the tale is telling.

=== Page 14 ===
Never weary of the ever joyous song,
Heart and voice united bear along.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend!
Foe within and out repelling.

War's alarum has but lately died away,
    Yet the echo rolls around us.
But the patriot host has overthrown the sway
    Of the haughty pow'r that bound us.
Freedom dwells where freedom never dwelt before,
Cries and groans shall grieve the land no more.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend
Has the suff'ring captive found us.

Freedom dwells thro'out our own beloved land:
    Wide as heav'n arches o'er it.
Like the rising sun, the patriot's armed hand
    Clouds of wrong hath swept before it.
Sound aloud the joyous word from crag to crag,
Plant on ev'ry peak our starry flag.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend!
Guard and like a shrine adore it.

GOD SAVE THE QUEEN

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced.

This is the fifth Concert of the Twenty-third Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only. The New Season commences after this Concert.

Intending Subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s.; Class C, £1 11s. 6d. Entrance fee to each Class, 10s. 6d.

Class A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 TICKETS.

Class B.—Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 TICKETS.

Class C.—Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 TICKETS.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.

Melbourne.
