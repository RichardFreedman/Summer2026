Programme

PART I

Part Song—"Consecration of Song"
Gustave Baldamus

SOLO—MR. J. GLADSTONE WRIGHT

Liedertafel

O'ER verdant meadows to the mountain height,
    In rays of morning do thou rise, O song,
And greet the sea and land, the day so bright,
    Rise to empyreal spheres where angels throng.

The solemn Sabbath bells intone the lay,
    That sweetly with the sylvan choir doth chime;
The brook doth gush, and thund'ring far away
    Roll hoary, crumbling rocks, e'en old as time.

Sublime, divine the pow'r of melodies
    That wakes the heart to worthiness and grace.
Sound forth o'er earth, ye wondrous harmonies,
    Resound in song throughout all time and space.

    Rejoice, O human-kind!
The pow'r of tone o'er life doth shed
    A happy ray of light.
Like sunshine spreading forth from zone to zone,
    So song shall quicken, waking pure delight.

Romanza—"Si tu Savais"    M. W. Balfe
Signor Attilio Buzzi

Cavatina—"Convien Partir"  Donizetti
(La Figlia del Reggimento)
Miss Juliet Flegeltaub

Cantata—"God and Fatherland" W. Tschirch
(TRANSLATED FROM THE GERMAN BY J. O. BROADLEY)

Liedertafel

From the clear blue arch of heaven
    Beams the sun with splendour bright.
Let our songs of praise be given
    To the God of life and light.
Great and wonderful art Thou!
Sovereign Lord, to Thee we bow.

Grand as organ nobly pealing,
    Let this vow to Heaven ascend;
Heart and voice the power revealing,
    Verse and harmony to blend.
Native country, native tongue,
Bind our hearts in union strong.

Word and weapon nobly plighted,
    To defend thee and to save;
We, thy children, stand united,
    Glorious shall thy banner wave.
Life we pledge at thy command,
O, thou dearest Fatherland.

Honour to the man who foldeth
    Truth and beauty to his breast;
But on him who sacred holdeth
    Woman's love let blessing rest.
Curse the dastard who doth prove
Traitor to a woman's love.

In full chorus now uniting,
    Pledged to love, be all agreed;
Friendship's sacred name now plighting,
    True to be in word and deed;
Swear by love to faithful stand;
Honour God and Fatherland.

Part Song—"The Miller's Daughter" Hartel
Liedertafel

STREAM and mill, your murmurs cease,
Sounds, to silence all decline;
Now, ye woodbirds, rest in peace,
Or in this my burthen join,
She is mine, she is mine; she has said she is mine.

Lovely springtime, thee I welcome merrily,
All thy flowrets blooming smile so cheerily,
Now no more, with care o'ergrown,
Feels my beating heart alone.
She is mine, yes, the miller's daughter now is mine.

INTERVAL OF FIVE MINUTES.

PART II

Cantata—"To the Sons of Art" Mendelssohn
Liedertafel

O Sons of Art, man's dignity to you is given:
    Preserve it man!
It falls with you, with you ascends to heaven!
The hallowed themes of Magian dreams,
Founded in Wisdom's vast creation,
Gliding like rivers find their ocean,
    That great harmonious plan.

Eternal truth, tho' oft rejected,
Exists not ever unprotected;
She finds a refuge with the tuneful throng.
She there appears in all her glory,
Mighty when veil'd in mystic story;
She wakes the lay of lofty voices,
And over all her foes rejoices,
Her vengeance flashing, peals in song.

To your free mother homage render,
Boldly to gain her height aspire;
Enthron'd she dwells in radiant splendour
No other crown than hers desire.
While you her thousand paths are tracing
Press onward, keeping Truth in sight;
Come altogether, stand embracing
Before the Throne where paths unite!

Part Song—"The Mariner's Return" Haeckler
Liedertafel

Pull away, boys, steer for the cove,
Yonder lie dreaming the hearts that we love.
Let us now banish all trouble and sorrow,
We shall have cause to be joyful to-morrow.
Glittering moonbeams bespangle the deep,
O'er the wide ocean the winds are asleep.

Storms are o'er, and land is in sight,
Calmly we glide thro' the waters to-night.
Many a heart will to-morrow be leaping,
Which in the bosom is quietly sleeping.
Rowing and singing we draw near the shore,
Where there are joys for us seamen in store.

Miss Juliet Flegeltaub

Part Song—"Martyrs of the Arena" De Rille
Liedertafel

Great Caesar, with our dying breath,
    Thus we hail thee!
The body thou canst doom to death,
But the soul shall hold fast her faith.
O Caesar, with our dying breath,
    Thus we hail thee!
Great lord of life and death!

    See, the town keeps holiday to-day,
The circus in festive array!
Now raise a merry shout to the gods,
With cymbals clashing, and trumpets blowing.
Before them the stern lictors' rods
See the consuls in crimson glowing.
See the pale Vestals' white robes flowing,
All attend till great Caesar nods.

    And we, all amid the dread arena,
Naked, defenceless, in God our sole reliance,
We hear with calm defiance
The roaring lion and hyena,
Soon to be our living tomb.

    Heard ye that ringing cheer?
They open now the cage,
And the tiger and panther in their rage,
They come madly bounding along.
Brethren, be strong!
Lift up your heart in prayer and song.

    God of the martyr and the slave,
O Christ, who hast triumphed over death!
Come, oh come, thy suff'ring saints to save.
Now they draw their parting breath!

    'Tis the hour of joyful compensation,
Hark! it greets us from eternity.
Now comes the long-looked-for salvation,
Now dawns the day of liberty.

=== Page 13 ===
And when the life-blood is pouring,
And day is dark'ning into night,
O living God, to Thee our souls are soaring,
And death is the dawning of endless light.

Cello Solo—"Drinking Song" Dunkler
Herr Theo. Liebe

Part Song—
(a) "Mariner's Hymn to the Dioscuri" Schubert-Plumpton
(b) "Wanderer's Song" Schumann-Plumpton

Liedertafel

(a) Dioscuri! shining twin stars,
    Ye who light us o'er the ocean;
Mighty helpers, guide us safely,
    Still the waves to gentle motion.

Tho' with firm hearts, fear unknowing,
    Undismayed with storms contending,
We would see your mild rays beaming,
    Hope and courage to us lending.

This strong oar that bears us onward,
    O'er the waves your rule obeying,
At your shrine we then will offer,
    At your altar homage paying.

=== Page 14 ===
Again fill your glasses with bright sparkling wine,
Once more will I pledge ye, dear comrades of mine!
Farewell, hoary mountains and dear native home,
Farewell! to a far distant land I must roam.

The bright sun above us at rest does not stand,
His course he fulfils over ocean and land;
The billows disperse and recede from the shore,
The storm rushes onward its fury to pour.

With swift-speeding pinion the bird flies away,
Still singing and soaring it pours out its lay.
Thus youth's glowing visions will urge to the race,
Thus earth wanders on thro' regions of space.

The birds of his own land the wanderer greet,
Its flowr's meet his eye with their odour so sweet;
They bring to his heart thoughts of home and of love,
The meadow beneath and the blue sky above.

He dreams that once more he his fatherland sees,
And sweet scents of home life come borne on the breeze;
Thus love e'er will follow the lov'd one afar,
And shine through the distance his one guiding star.

GOD SAVE THE QUEEN
