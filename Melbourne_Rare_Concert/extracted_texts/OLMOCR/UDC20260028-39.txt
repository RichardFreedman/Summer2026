Programme

PART II

Introduction to Acts 1 and 2 "Lohengrin"
Orchestra Wagner

"The Water-lily" op. 11
H. Goetz—1840-76
CANTATA FOR MALE VOICES AND SOPRANO SOLO

Miss Lilian Tree, Liedertafel and Orchestra

In evening stillness lies the lake,
    And mirrors the waning glow of the west,
Their thirst come timorous deer to slake,
    And swans fly, encircling its tranquil breast.

The fisher boy unfastens his boat,
    The plash of his oar thro' the silence steals:
In Fancy's realm does he appear to float,
    He knows not why in his heart thus he feels.

Around him whisper the darkling waves,
    And sing in their motion, far and wide,
The water a dim wooded margin laves,
    And the spectre-like mountains tower in pride.

=== Page 8 ===
The cooling breath of the evening air,
    It plays thro' his locks all golden and bright.
A fragrance comes from the flow'rets fair,
    As low they are bending for sleep at night.

But the water-lily is yet awake,
    Though nodding in slumb'rous mood is she;
She bends unto him from her native lake,
    Tho' evening invites her at rest to be.

His dream is dispelled, and he quits his oar:
    His face is on fire with longing confessed;
He stoops from the boat, the spot to explore,
    He plucks off a lily, thus decking his breast.

But hark! with fury the forest shakes,
    The lake uplifts its roaring on high,
And sudden over a mountain breaks
    The ruddy full moon, so wrathful of eye.

And the lake exclaims, with heart deeply moved,
    "Why trouble the children within their home?"

And the forest complains, "These long have I loved,
    Why force these delightful sisters to roam?"
But the red-glowing moon then fiercely cries,
    "How darest thou to address my bride?"
And in terror the boy with tears homeward flies,
    That there, with his mother, his grief he may hide.

    "O mother, the roar of the lake I heard,
    Against me the forest with fury shook,
Before me, in anger, the full moon appeared,
    O mother, I but a lily took!"

(b) Pizzicato "Sylvia"      Delibes

"Softly Sighs"  Weber
Scena from "Der Freischütz"
(WITH ORCHESTRAL ACCOMPANIMENT)
Miss Lilian Tree

"Forsaken"  Roschat
Liedertafel

ALONE! ah, forsaken!
    Forsaken am I!
Not a maiden smiles on me,
    They all pass me by.
Far off to the church-yard
    In sorrow I stray,
And lowly there, kneeling,
    In sorrow I pray.

A mound in the green-wood,
    White flow'rets bloom there,
And softly she sleepeth
    Who once was so fair.
And since my own loved one
    From earth scenes hath gone,
'Mid life and its pleasure
    I am always alone.

=== Page 10 ===
"Night in the Forest"    Schubert
(PART SONG WITH ACCOMPANIMENT OF HORNS)

Liedertafel

Ever sweet art thou, O Night!
    In the forest sweeter far,
Where thy starry eyes gleam bright,
    Where thy soft enchantments are!
Where the leaves, like fairy goblets,
    Overflow with dewy wine,
And the moon's unclouded radiance
    Fills each glade with light divine.

Murmuring breezes sing of thy pleasures,
Silvery moonbeams weave thee thy treasures;
All whom thy lips have silently press'd,
Closing their eyes, sink sweetly to rest!

But who would sleep on night so clear?
Arise! the horn rings far and near,
And wakens with its gladsome sound
Each slumberer in the groves around.
    Wake! wake! wake!

It echoes the woods and the mountain tops o'er,
The birds, all awaking, think night is no more;
The wandering deer in affright flee away,
They fancy it heralds the dawn of the day;
The lords of the forest shout loud in their might,
The fountain awakes with a song of delight!

Then sing we as we wander,
    "The woods are the home of the Night!"
And echo answers yonder,
    "The woods, thy home, O Night!"
Yes, here within thy forest home,
    We'll greet thee, lovely, lovely Night,
Where smiling from thy heav'nly dome,
    Thou reign'st in all thy might!

Hungarian Rhapsodie No. 4
Orchestra
Liszt

"Gondola Song"
J. E. Schmöller

Mr. Moyle and Liedertafel

Come love, the sea invites us,
    Our barque is on the shore;
Say now if I shall sing thee
    Of wond'rous deeds of yore,
Of heroes slain on battle plain?

Solo—Oh, no! oh, no! in sweeter strains my song must flow.

    Come love, the twilight darkens,
        The stars now greet our eyes;
Say now if I shall sing thee
        Of Freedom's high emprise?
Of patriots grand, of Fatherland?

Solo—Oh, no! oh, no! in sweeter strains my song must flow.

    Come love, my heart is longing
        For thy dear presence bright;
Say now if I shall sing thee
        Of mirth and gay delight?
Shall themes of joy my lute employ?

Solo—Oh, no! oh, no! in sweeter strains my song must flow.

    Come love, let us together
        Sail o'er the waters clear;
Say now with legend olden
        Shall I enchant thine ear?
Some magic store of fairy lore?

Solo—Oh, no! oh, no! in sweeter strains my song must flow.

=== Page 12 ===
"Die Allmacht" Schubert—Liszt

(WITH ORCHESTRAL ACCOMPANIMENT)

Miss Lilian Tree and Liedertafel

Great is Jehovah, the Lord!
The heavens and the earth proclaim His wondrous might;
    In storms his dread accents are heard,
In the wild forest stream He calls by night;
        Great is Jehovah, the Lord!

Thou hearest it in the green budding boughs of the forest,
    See'st it in the corn fields that glow like gold,
In flowers ye may see it in bright varied hues,
    And in the stars still His marvellous power behold.

Sounds His voice in the dread thunder roll,
    And flames in the lightning on its swift blinding flight,
And each beating heart, every soul
    Feels His wondrous power and might;
        The eternal God, the Lord!

Thou to Him dost look for aid,
    In hope to gain His love and grace,
Trust in Him, be not afraid,
    He ne'er will turn away His face.
        Great is Jehovah, the Lord!

PART II

THE Second Portion of the Concert will take the form of a CONVERSAZIONE in the Main Hall, during which the Orchestra will play a Selection of the following Works, viz.:

1. Overture "William Tell" - - Rossini
2. Pizzicato "Liebesliedchen" - - Taubert
3. "Midsummer Night's Dream" Mendelssohn (Scherzo, Nocturne, and Wedding March)
4. "Ukrainischer Tanz" (Russian Dance) S. Noskowski
5. Overture "Merry Wives of Windsor" Nicolai

GOD SAVE THE QUEEN

Note.—During the Conversazione Refreshments will be obtainable.
