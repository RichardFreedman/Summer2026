PART I

Cantata—The Viking's Last Voyage
(BARITONE SOLO, MR. HORACE E. STEVENS) CHADWICK

Liedertafel

NORTHWARD from Denmark came Eric and Jorund,
    To Upsal came sailing to win back their own
From Hakon the Sea-king, who, conquering Sweden,
    Their kinsman had slain, and had seized on his throne,

King Hakon of Sweden was strong, brave and fearless,
    And though half his warriors were roaming the sea,
And cruising as vikings from Norway to Bretland,
    As fierce as the tempest, as wild and as free,

Yet fought he the brothers with spirit undaunted,
    Until by his sword-stroke King Eric fell dead;
Until with his forces all broken and scattered,
    Back to his warships King Jorund had fled.

But Hakon, the fearless, was mortally wounded,
    His men, dead and dying, were strewn on the shore,
The tide was fast ebbing and so was his life-blood,
    He knew that by nightfall he'd enter death's door.

=== Page 8 ===
Sadly, yet bravely, he turned his glance seaward.
    He saw his great warship at rest on the strand;
The ship that had borne him through storm and through battle,
    Her helm ever faithful beneath his firm hand.

Then all the old mem'ries came thronging and throbbing:
    Of cruises so many, so long and so far,
Of storms on the ocean, of salt spray a-dashing,
    Of fights in the Baltic, of wounds, and of scar.

Of midnight in summer, the sky flushed with crimson,
    The waters reflecting the northern light's bars.
Of loyal companions, and telling of sagas,
    While sailing on swiftly beneath the bright stars.

"And now a long cruise we shall sail for the last time."
    The dying King said to those gathered near,
"His heroes with Hakon will set out undaunted,
    To death as to battle we go without fear.

"Launch then the warship, her deck pile with dead wood.
    And thereon rest gently my battle-men dead,
Your King place among them all clad in his armour,
    And bright shield untarnished laid under his head.

"Free let the helm be, set all the flags flying,
    The great anchor hang in its place at the rail,
And then fire the death pile, thus passing in glory
    Straight east to Walhalla and Odin we'll sail."

Just as the low sun had gilded with splendour
    The wild rocky islands that bounded the bay,
And then sunk in crimson behind the far mountains,
    The soul of King Hakon from earth passed away.

In the grey gloaming the moorings were severed.
    The fresh wind blew off shore, the fire leaped up red,
In clear flame a-burning between the dark islands,
    Straight eastward to Odin, King Hakon's ship sped.

Part Song—On the Water
(WITH HUMMING ACCOMPANIMENT)

Mr. A. W. Jack and Liedertafel

Soft the glassy waves are flowing,
  Gently, gently let us glide;
Golden stars and sunset glowing
  Flash upon the silent tide.
As the day and night are blending,
  Floating o'er the waves along,
From the depths below ascending
  Comes a strange and ghostly song.

The king's enchanted daughter
  Has swept the golden strings;
And under the depths of the water
  She sweetly, sadly sings.
She sings of echoing forest,
  She sings of flowery plain.
And how she, in longing and sorrow,
  Thereunder must ever remain.

She sings of days in childhood,
  Of joyous, lovely May;
And how they are now all departed,
  How that has faded away.
It floated over the water,
  And died away on the air,
That song of a joy now departed,
  That sorrowful song of despair.

Cavaliers' Songs—
(a) Boot, saddle, to horse, and away
(b) King Charles
(c) Marching along
C. VILLIERS STANFORD
(BARITONE SOLO, MR. HORACE E. STEVENS)

Liedertafel
(a) Boot, saddle, to horse, and away!
Rescue my castle before the hot day
Brightens to blue from its silvery grey.
Boot, saddle, to horse, and away!

Ride past the suburbs, asleep as you'd say,
Many's the friend there will listen and pray
God's luck to the gallants that strike up the lay.
Boot, saddle, to horse, and away!

=== Page 11 ===
Forty miles off, like a roebuck at bay,
Flouts Castle Brancepeth the Roundheads' array,
Who laughs "Good fellows ere this, by my fay?"
    Boot, saddle, to horse, and away!

Who? My wife Gertrude, that, honest and gay,
Laughs when you talk of surrendering. Nay!
I've better counsellors, what counsel they?
    Boot, saddle, to horse, and away!

(b) King Charles! and who'll do him right now?
King Charles! and who's ripe for the fight now?
    Give a rouse
Here's in Hell's despite now
    King Charles.

Who gave me the goods that went since?
Who raised me the house that sank once?
Who helped me to gold I spent since?
Who found me in wine you drank once?
    King Charles.

For whom used my boy George quaff else
By the old fool's side that begot him?
For whom did he cheer and quaff else
While Noll's damn'd troopers shot him?
    King Charles.

(c) Kentish Sir Byng stood for his king,
Bidding the crop-headed Parliament swing,
And pressing a troop, unable to stoop,
And see the rogues flourish and honest men droop,
Marched them along, fifty score strong,
Great-hearted gentlemen singing this song.

=== Page 12 ===
God for King Charles! Pym and such carles
To the devil that prompts their treasonous parles!
Cavallers up! lips from the cup!
Hands from the pastry, nor bite take or sup,
    'Till you're marching along, fifty score strong.
    Great-hearted gentlemen singing this song.

Hampden to hell, and his obsequies knell.
Serve Hazelrig Fiennes and young Harry as well.
England good cheer. Rupert is near,
Kentish, and loyalists keep we not here.
    Marching along, &c.

Then God for King Charles! Pym and his snarls
To the devil that pricks on their treasonous parles,
Hold by the right, you double your might,
So onward to Nottingham, fresh for the fight.
    March we along, fifty score strong.
    Great-hearted gentlemen singing this song.

PART II

Grand Double Chorus from Oedipus at Colonos
MENDELSSOHN

(WITH PIANO AND ORGAN ACCOMPANIMENT)

Liedertafel

Ah, were I on yonder plain,
Where now our undaunted sons
Stand arm'd for the hostile fray;
There, where the shrine of Phoebus gleams,
And torches are burning;
Where mortals bow down to the awe-inspiring pair,
Whose hallow'd rites are mystic deeds,
Wrought by Eumolpidan priests,
And never breath'd to mortal ear.
    There, glowing with ardour,
    Panting to rush against the foe,
        See, see all our legions.
With Theseus they wave their swords, and cry,
    Rescue the sisters!

    Or westward perchance they rush.
    Where snow-cover'd mountains rise
        O'er Gea's enamell'd meads;
There dancing cars and prancing steeds
    Will clash in the onset!

=== Page 14 ===
There on the foe soon will impending vengeance fall,
    For Theseus leads our valiant bands:
    See their arms and armour gleaming
Bright as the trappings that grace their steeds!
        Now proudly exulting,
They, who honour the deeds of sage Minerva
And Rhea's great son, earth-encircling ocean's king,
        Press to the battle!

    Do they await, pursue or fight?
        My heart thrills with hope
That they will soon rescue the captive maid,
Who hath endur'd, and still endures her kindred's woe.
    This day, Jove will restore her,
Crown your deeds, heroes, with glory!
Like a dove high soaring,
Sped along by the storm-wind,
I, from the verge of a cloud,
Would look down on the contest,
And see my country's cause triumphant!

    Dread Pow'r, that fillest heav'n's high throne,
O all-seeing Jove, defend our great ruler!
        On him the victor's palm bestow!
        With verdant laurels deck his brow!
        We call on thee, Pallas Athene!
        Mighty hunter, Pcebus, hear us!
And thy sister, fleet in chase, whose dart unerring
        Pierceth the far distant hart:
        O come, defend our guardians!
        We pray ye, aid our native heroes!

Serenade—The Drowsy Woods
(TENOR SOLO, MR. G. J. JOHNSON)
STORCH

Liedertafel

The drowsy woods are wrapped in shadow;
    The dreamy clouds hang o'er the lake;
The quiet moon sleeps on the meadow;
    But love now watches for thy sake.

The ocean now in silence slumbers,
    Unheard the billows gently break,
And so to thee, in whispered numbers,
    Comes love, who watches for thy sake.

Then rouse thee not, tho' love be singing,
    Or from thine eyes their slumbers shake,
If in thy soul the tones are ringing
    Of love, who watches for thy sake.

Part Song—Loyal Song
KÜCKEN

Liedertafel

FREEDOM dwells thro'out our own beloved land:
    Up to heav'n its voice is swelling,
From the mountain heights afar to ocean strand
    Ev'ry breeze the tale is telling.

=== Page 17 ===
Never weary of the ever joyous song,
Heart and voice united bear along.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend!
Foe within and out repelling.

War's alarum has but lately died away,
    Yet the echo rolls around us.
But the patriot host has overthrown the sway
    Of the haughty pow'r that bound us.
Freedom dwells where freedom never dwelt before,
Cries and groans shall grieve the land no more.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend
Has the suff'ring captive found us.

Freedom dwells thro'out our own beloved land,
    Wide as heav'n arches o'er it.
Like the rising sun, the patriot's armed hand
    Clouds of wrong hath swept before it.
Sound aloud the joyous word from crag to crag.
Plant on ev'ry peak our starry flag.
    Loyal to the end!
    Ready to defend!
Guard and like a shrine adore it!

Memoranda

Subscribers and friends are reminded that the Subscription to this Society has been materially reduced, and the entrance fee abolished.

This is the first Concert of the Thirtieth Season. Three are given to ladies and gentlemen, and two to gentlemen only.

Intending subscribers may join Class A, £1 1s.; Class B, £2 2s.; Class C, £1 11s. 6d.

CLASS A.—Two tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—8 TICKETS.

CLASS B.—Five tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and two for each Concert to gentlemen only—19 TICKETS.

CLASS C.—Three tickets for each Concert to ladies and gentlemen, and one for each Concert to gentlemen only—11 TICKETS.

LIFE SUBSCRIBERS.—The sum of Ten Guineas will secure for life the privileges of Class A.

Subscribers joining during the year are only charged pro rata.

Subscribers' tickets are transferable by endorsement.

Extra tickets, which are only sold to Subscribers, are 4s. for Concerts to ladies and gentlemen, and 2s. 6d. for Concerts to gentlemen only.

The Hon. Secretary will be glad to receive nominations from ladies and gentlemen desiring to become Subscribers.

The Christmas Carol Smoke Concert will be given in the Athenaeum Hall, on Monday evening, 22nd December.
