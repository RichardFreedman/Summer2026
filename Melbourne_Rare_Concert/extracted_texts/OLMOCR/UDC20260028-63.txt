Programme

Under the Baton of the Hon. Conductor,
Professor Marshall-Hall.

Part I.

Madrigals—

(a) Now is the month of Maying
THOMAS MORLEY
(1595)

(b) Awake! Sweet Love
JOHN DOWLAND
(1597)

Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.

(a) Now is the month of Maying, when merry lads are playing,
    Fa la la, etc.
Each with his bonny lass, a dancing on the grass.
    Fa la la, etc.
The Spring clad all in gladness, doth laugh at Winter's sadness,
    Fa la la, etc.
And to the Bagpipes sound, the nymphs tread out their ground.
    Fa la la, etc.
Fie then, why sit we musing, youth's sweet delight refusing?
    Fa la la, etc.
Say, dainty nymphs, and speak, shall we play barley break?
    Fa la la, etc.

(b) Awake, sweet love! thou art return'd;
    My heart, which long in absence mourn'd,
    Lives now in perfect joy.
Only herself hath seemed fair,
    She only I could love,
She only drove me to despair
    When she unkind did prove.

Madrigal—
Matona, Lovely Maiden
ORLANDO LASSUS
(1520-1594)

Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.

MATONA, lovely maiden,
O listen to the song
I sing beneath thy window,
    While night clouds roll along,
        Dong, dong, derry, derry, etc.,

I pray you hear my ditty,
    'Tis sweet and not too long,
    'Tis pointed, if not witty,
        And sharpen'd like a prong.
        Dong, dong, derry, derry, etc.

The words of choicest tissue,
    To shoot love's aim, belong,
Then from your window issue,
    Or else you do me wrong.
        Dong, dong, derry, derry, etc.

You'll say if once you catch them,
    And note their fine ding dong,
Petrarcha could not match them,
    They are so sweet and strong.
        Dong, dong, derry, derry, etc.

But if you think the measure,
    Should to all scorn belong,
A more complying treasure
    I'll choose from out the throng.
        Dong, dong, derry derry, etc.

Part Song—Marienlied
MICHAEL PRAETORIUS (1571-1621)

Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.
(Translated from the German, for the Liedertafel, by Herr H. W. Puttman).

THERE is a Rose unfolded,
    Out of a tender shoot;
As sung to us of olden,
    From Jesse sprung its root.
It bloom'd for our delight
    All 'mid the frosts of winter,
Nigh on the noon of night.

That Roselet blest of Heaven,
    Isaiah did forshow,
By Mary to us given,
    The virgin pure as snow.
She, thro' God's holy might
    Gave birth unto an infant,
Nigh on the noon of night.

Flora gave me Fairest Flowers
JOHN WILBYE (1598)

Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.

FLORA gave me fairest flowers,
None so fair in Flora's treasure.
These I placed in Phillis' bowers,
She was pleas'd and she's my pleasure,
Smiling meadows seem to say,

Madrigal—
April is in my Mistress' Face
THOMAS MORLEY
(1594)
Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.

APRIL is in my Mistress' face,
And July in her eyes hath place,
Within her bosom is September,
But in her heart is cold December.

O were it July all the year,
Then April showr's I would not fear,
Nor blight that falleth in September,
Nor frost that chills in cold December.

Madrigal—
Down in a Flow'ry Vale
CONSTANTINE FESTA
(1541)
Liedertafel and Associated Ladies' Choir.

DOWN in a flow'ry vale, all on a summer morning,
Phillis I spied, fair nature's self adorning;
Swiftly on wings of love, I flew to meet her,
Coldly she welcomed me when I did greet her.

I warbled thus my ditty:
O shepherdess have pity,
And hear a faithful lover, his passion true discover;
Ah! why art thou to me so cruel?
Then straight replied my jewel—
If gold thou hast, fond youth, 'twill speed thy suing,
But if thy purse be empty, come not to me a wooing.

=== Page 10 ===
Soon as I careless stray'd, fond youth, with eyes averted,
Phillis I met, by all the swains deserted;
Swift she (tho' late so coy) then flew to meet me,
My back I turn'd, all deal to her entreaty;

She warbled thus her ditty:
O shepherd, now have pity,
And to your faithful lover your passion true discover.
Then did I, cold and haughty view her,
And thus replied unto her—
The love that's won by gold will prove undoing;
So since my purse is empty, I go no more a wooing.
