Programme

PART I

Cantata "Harald's Bridal Voyage"
Heinrich Hofmann
(WITH ORCHESTRAL ACCOMPANIMENT)
Solos by Mr. A. H. Gee and Mr. Henry Rofe

Liedertafel

1.—The Departure

Up, up, and away, every sail widely spanned,
The anchor set free, and the yards all bemanned.
Yohio, yohi! wind, bear us a tide,
Guard us and the hero when storm billows ride.

High swells his heart, and he longs for the bride,
Her all of his soul and his life doth confide,
Beauteous will adorn her with purple and gold,
With gems and pearls as his queen enfold.

An island hides her in ocean afar,
There brightly she shines as a radiant star,
The Maid of the Sea, demons guard day and night,
But our hero will win her all valiant in might.

Yohio, yohi! wind, bear us a tide,
Guard us and the hero when storm billows ride.

=== Page 8 ===
2.—On the Strand

An Old Sailor:

Depart, farewell! thou'lt see this port aye never,
    Your fatherland greets ye the last to-day,
Soon raging storm as foam the ship shall sever,
    Ye all shall be of fish and fowl the prey.

    Storm birds I see are flying,
    The wolf to forest hieing;
    Signs these that hell aye keepeth,
    When low the eagle sweepeth.

These signs full oft I read descrying,
    But younger folk their doubt will cast,
High rolls the tide, the winds are sighing,
    What is to be, must be, at last.

    Shout then and revel,
    Soon thy song shall die away,
    The raven croaks, deep caverns yawn,
    Thy fatherland greets ye the last to-day.

3.—On the Sea

Beauteous prize our zeal engageth,
    Be thou gracious, seaman's fate!
Lead us safe though tempest rageth,
    Back to native port elate.

Where we wander, where abiding
    In the hand of God we are,
On, great ship, the waves dividing,
    Aye before goes God's fair star.

So we're full of valour daring,
    Fear naught deep nor storm award,
Joy all earthly treasure sharing,
    For the Lord our house doth guard.

=== Page 9 ===
Harald's Song:

From the sea all darkly flowing,
    High the sun ascends and higher,
Fills the mead with radiance glowing,
    Wakes the Nereidian choir.
Love's bright star is beck'ning me,
    O! I'll follow, follow thee!

Where the cooling springs are plying,
    Lone she waketh, dream-bedight,
There I'll listen for her sighing
    Through the love-enraptured night.
I will listen for her sighing
    Through the love-enraptured night.

Swell, ye sails, and call, ye billows,
    Roar and blow, ye strong winds wild,
That I with my warriors daring,
    Soon may reach the darling child.

Where the cooling springs are plying,
    Lone she waketh, dream-bedight,
There I'll listen for her sighing
    Through the love-enraptured night.

4.—The Storm

The God of Storms and the Demons of the South Sea:

Ye storms there on high,
    And fiends of the deep,
O tarry ye not,
    Where'er ye may sleep!

For battle prepared,
    Your God calleth ye,
Roll on as the thunder,
    And plough ye the sea.

=== Page 10 ===
O shiver the masts,
    Shake the bed of the sea,
Make known to them all
    Sons of hell ye be.

On the Ship:

Hark, storm-spirits dark are nighing,
    Threat'ning clouds all downward fly,
        For the storm spirit's nigh!
Reef the sails, the yards all lower,
    On the deck then every man.

Billow rises wild on billow,
    Piping raves the furious wind,
Is the anchor fast and steady?
    Still our hope to that we bind.

Woe! The mast broke, shrouds all shriven,
    Cleft the great ship's mighty bow,
Flash on flash red lightning driven
    Wraps in flame the anchor tow.

On and on the storm is waging,
    'Gainst the hellish rocks they dip.
See the furies, hear their raging,
    In the great deep sinks the ship.

The God of Storms:

To the deep she went down!
Come, O sun, here downward pour thee,
Ne'er that ship will sail before thee.

=== Page 11 ===
The Demons:

Victoria! That ship we sank for ever,
    Of shameless robbers quick revenge obtain
O'er us hath mortal had victory never,
    Ancient and mighty ocean race we reign.

Ill fares who seeks the prize 'tis ours to guard,
    And woe! shall sink in deepest wave.
Who his desire and hope our waves award,
    Earns for his daring bold an early grave.

5.—Closing Song

The Spirits of the Sea:

Slumber soft in deep sea lying,
    Lulled by billow-throng for aye.
Our ear heareth all your sighing,
    When ye plaint to plaint reply.

Calmly moon and stars are bending
    From high heaven unto the wave.
Far and wide night shadow blending,
    God doth guard your silent grave.

And the spirit choir are mourning
    With the nymphs thy fate's decree,
And the spirit choir are saying:
    Slumber soft in deepest sea!

Pianoforte Solo: Concerto in E Flat Major Beethoven
Miss Florence Menk-Meyer.

PART II

Serenade

(TENOR SOLO Mr. W. Clarence Fraser)

Liedertafel

The drowsy woods are wrapt in shadow;
    The dreamy clouds hang o'er the lake;
The quiet moon sleeps on the meadow;
    But love now watches for thy sake.

The ocean now in silence slumbers,
    Unheard the billows gently break,
And so to thee, in whispered numbers
    Comes love, who watches for thy sake.

Then rouse thee not, tho' love be singing,
    Or from thine eyes their slumbers shake,
If in thy soul the tones are ringing,
    Of love, who watches for thy sake.

Song

"L'Estasi"

Miss O'Shannessy

Selections from "La Damnation de Faust"
Berlioz

Boys' Choir, Victorian Orchestra
AND Liedertafel

1.—Rakocsky March

2.—Easter Hymn

CHRIST is risen from the dead.
    Has broken the tomb,
    Gladly hail the token.
    Sin's fetters are broken,
Reversed is the doom,
Now the Master hath ascended,
    Rejoice for your bondage is o'er,
And the reign of sin is ended,
    Praise him for evermore!

    Alas! those He loved can but languish,
    Suffer mid pain and annoy.
    Master, we envy Thy joy,
Forget not the depth of our anguish.
    Hosanna!

3.—Danse des Sylphes

4.—Chorus of Soldiers and Students

Soldiers.

Fortresses defended with strong ramparts high,
Fair maidens who daringly love's power defy,
Of conquest we're certain soon as we take the field,
We reck not of danger, to us they must yield.
At the sound of the trumpet we soldiers so
To revel or battle at once march away.

=== Page 14 ===
Students.

Jam nox stellata vel amina pandit,
Nunc bibendum et amandum est,
Vita brevis fugax que voluptas,
    Gaudeamus igitur.

Nobis sub ridente luna,
Per urbem quaerentes puellas eamus.
Ut cras fortunati Cæsares dicamus,
Veni, vidi, vici, gaudeamus igitur.

Song    "Bedouin Love Song"    Coombs
Mr. A. H. Gee

Part Song    "Farewell"    Hinkel

Ah! must we part for ever?
One kiss before we sever.
Beside my bold commander
I far away must wander.
    Farewell, farewell, beloved maid!

I ne'er again may greet thee,
On sunny pathway meet thee;
The foe will stand before me,
And battle clouds roll o'er me.
    Farewell, farewell, beloved maid!

I long for thee when parted,
Weep thou for me true hearted!
When life's last pulses flutter,
My latest breath shall utter,
    Farewell, farewell, beloved maid!

=== Page 15 ===
Vocal Waltz "Blue Danube" (By request)
(ARRANGED BY ELSASSER) Strauss

Liedertafel and Orchestra

Why should we sigh, and mirth decry,
And why from content seek banishment?
With care why alloy the cup of joy,
To vainly repent o'er days misspent?
No! cherish the hours, while youth is ours;
And give not to care life's better share;
    But the sage old device
    Let us prize,
That this world's both good and fair!
Though in lane and street,
Oftsoe'er you meet
    Gloomy faces, sorrow's traces,
Think that evermore.
When the clouds pass o'er,
Lips will smile that smil'd before, Once more!
Ever thus sweet Spring
Weaves his flow'ry ring,
    Gladness wooing, world renewing;
And like airy sprite,
Ceaseless day and night,
Joy doth weave a new delight, With micht.
    Her shapes are manifold
    Her voices all untold,
    She dwells in ev'ry place
    Where pure hearts may follow
        Her fairy trace.
She roams on sunlit hills,
Or lurks by babbling rills,
Or rocks on ocean's breast,
    Or sings where the woodlands
        In shadow rest.

=== Page 16 ===
But, oh! sweet is the image of joy,
    In human face that dwells,
When love new-born, of its pure ecstasy
    In humid glances tells.

O'er joy-illumined eye
Night spreads no starless sky;
And love-filled heart can hear
    The chime of earth's joy-bells
        Sound far and near.

O ye, that look through a darkening veil,
And ye, faint souls, that life's sorrows bewail,
Look up! and trust to Nature's decree:
    Where life is, love can be.

Come, haste your dull doleful creed to forsake,
To brighter faiths let your spirits awake,
Nor doubt that towards a heaven-lov'd end
    All joyful things will tend.

Then silence not the voice
That would in glee rejoice,
Nor dim in darksome night
Eyes made to bask in light;
Hush not the silv'ry note
That falls from Mirth's gay throat,
But when the maid thou hast,
    O hold her fast!

No wanton sprite is she,
That would avoided be,
But in each guileless breast
A gladly harbour'd guest;
Love that hath such bridesmaid,
By her more lovely's made—
Blest, where in cot or hall,
    Joy's footsteps fall.

=== Page 17 ===
And oft, when adrift in thought's rudderless boat,
    Calmly we float
    From life's noise remote,
Across the still waters her bright glances beam,
And bathe all in sunlight our dream.
Or when at Humanity's glorious shrine
    Men's hearts entwine
    In love half-divine,
'Tis joy that her glow to our pulses imparts,
And throbs in our grateful hearts.
    O rejoice! Heart and voice
    Raise to heav'n above, laud the Source of Love.
    He is good, great and wise;
    Wraft from earth to skies
    Hymns of gratitude!
Now to the gay dance's rhythmical sound
See the golden-haired hours circle round,
While our thoughts in harmonious rhyme
To their soul-stirring cadence keep time!
Stealing us far from the sorrows of life,
Shedding balm o'er its heart-paining strife,
Guiding gently to sweet spots of rest
For the heart that with gladness is blest!
    Joy and song
    Be our theme life-long,
    And in jocund strain
    Sound again the refrain!
    Sing and laugh, laugh and sing—
    Come what fate may bring!
    Let joy's paeans rise
    In glad songs to the skies!
        Then sing, yes sing, O sing!

GOD SAVE THE QUEEN.
