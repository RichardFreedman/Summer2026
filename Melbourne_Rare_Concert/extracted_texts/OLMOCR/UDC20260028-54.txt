SONGS
    a. The flower Girl
    b. The snowdrop
    c. Old Scots Cradle song
    d. Heaven, haven

Jasper B. Rooper

a.
I stand here all the day,
Calling my roses,
Under a summer sky,
"Oh! who will buy
My pretty posies,
My Lords and Ladies gay?"

Thirteen summers are dead
With all their roses.
Now call I down the street,
"Oh! buy my sweet,
My pretty posies!
My flowers white and red."

The wind sings all the day
Swaying my roses.
"Oh! come, my little one!
Come, heed the sun
And drop your posies,
My little one, and play."

I bend my weary head
Down to my roses,
"He would not be unkind
The gentle wind,
My pretty posies
But Oh, that I were dead!"

And still I stand all day
Calling my roses,
So old, so old am I.
"Oh! will you buy
My pretty posies,
My Lords and Ladies gay."

=== Page 3 ===
b. Close to the sod there can be seen
A thought of God in white and green.
Unmarried, unsoiled it left the clay,
Serene, unspoil'd it views the day.

It is so holy, yet so lowly,
Would you enjoy its grace and dower
And not destroy the living flower?
Then you must, please, fall on your knees.

c. O my deir hert, young Jesus sweit,
Prepare thy creddil in my spriet,
And I sall rock thee in my hert
And never mair from thee depart.

But I sall praise thee ever muir
With sangis sweit unto thy gloir.
The knees of my hert sall I bow
And sing that right Balulalow.

d. I have desired to go
Where springs not fail,
To fields where flies no sharp and sided hail,
And a few lilies blow.

And I have asked to be
Where no storms come,
Where green swell is in the Heavens dumb—
And out of the swing of the sea.
